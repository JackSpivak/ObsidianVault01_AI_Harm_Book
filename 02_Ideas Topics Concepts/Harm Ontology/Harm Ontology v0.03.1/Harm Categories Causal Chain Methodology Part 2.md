**Yes, several international standards and formal methodologies have rigorously vetted and adopted this exact causation chain.** While they use standardized technical vocabulary rather than the informal phrase "harmful \[X\]," the underlying sequence—**Source → Action/Trigger → Event → Condition/Context → Target/Consequence**—is the foundational architecture of modern risk, safety, and security standards.

1\. International Standards That Codify This Chain

**ISO/IEC 27005 & NIST SP 800-30 (Information Security Risk Management)**

These are the global benchmarks for cybersecurity and organizational risk assessment. Both standards legally and operationally vet a strict causal chain to identify risk:

* **Threat Source** *(Harmful Actor / Intent)*  
* **Threat Event / Action** *(Harmful Action)*  
* **Vulnerability / Predisposing Condition** *(Harmful Condition)*  
* **Security Incident / Event** *(Harmful Event / Situation)*  
* **Asset / Target & Impact** *(Harmful Target / Consequence)*

**ISO 31000 & IEC 31010 (Enterprise Risk Management & Bowtie Analysis)**

ISO 31000 is the overarching international standard for risk across all industries. Its companion standard, **IEC 31010**, formally codifies **Bowtie Analysis**—one of the most widely vetted causal chain models in aviation, energy, and healthcare:

* **Hazard / Risk Source** *(Latent Harmful Condition)*  
* **Threat / Initiating Cause** *(Harmful Actor \+ Action)*  
* **Top Event** *(Harmful Event — the exact point where control is lost)*  
* **Escalation Factors** *(Conditions that worsen the situation)*  
* **Consequence** *(Harmful Outcome)*

**ISO 12100 & ISO 14971 (Machinery & Medical Device Safety)**

In physical engineering and healthcare, regulators (such as the FDA and EU) require manufacturers to prove safety using this exact four-step causal sequence:

1. **Hazard** *(Potential source of harm)*  
2. **Hazardous Situation** *(Circumstance in which people or property are exposed to a hazard)*  
3. **Hazardous Event** *(Specific occurrence that triggers the harm)*  
4. **Harm** *(Physical injury or damage to health/property)*

2\. Vetted Academic & Intelligence Methodologies

Beyond ISO and NIST standards, three major methodologies have formally peer-reviewed and validated this causal structure:

* **STPA / STAMP (MIT / Systems-Theoretic Process Analysis):** Developed by Prof. Nancy Leveson at MIT, this methodology is the gold standard in aerospace, defense, and autonomous vehicle safety. It replaces linear blame with a vetted loop of **Unsafe Control Actions**, **Context/Conditions**, **System States (Situations)**, and **Losses**.  
* **The Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis (U.S. Department of Defense / NSA):** Peer-reviewed and published by defense researchers (Caltagirone, Pendergast, Betz), it mathematically models intrusion events as a connected graph of **Adversary**, **Capability**, **Infrastructure**, and **Victim**, bound together by **Causal Events** and **Meta-Features (Intent/Phase)**.  
* **CAPEC & MITRE ATT\&CK:** Maintained by MITRE and funded by the U.S. government, these are the industry-standard taxonomies that catalog thousands of real-world **Actors**, **Attack Patterns (Actions)**, **Weaknesses (Conditions)**, and **Impacts**.
