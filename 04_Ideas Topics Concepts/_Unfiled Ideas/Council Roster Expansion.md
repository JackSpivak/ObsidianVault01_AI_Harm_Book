# Council Roster Expansion

## Why this is distinct
- It is a roster: the full set of additional councils the author listed, grouped by domain. The mechanics of how councils work are on a separate card, [[Council Dialogue Mechanics - Question-Led Councils]].

## Anticipated direction
- Group the councils into families (editorial and arts, human experience, religious and wisdom traditions, institutions and governance, science and systems, nature and ecology, storytelling and history).
- Assign each council a question set and a role (weighing in on types of harm, or checking uncanny narrative).

## Conceptual territory
- Extends the council sections of [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]] and the editorial-council idea in [[Embodied Experience Councils for Uncanny Detection]].

## Summary
- Editorial and arts: writers, cinematographers, concert musicians, athletes, comedians, clowns, artists, storytellers.
- Human experience: psychotherapists, somatic and body workers, sex therapists, a council of love, a council of happiness, a council of children and innocence, a council of education and educators, a council of care and care organizations.
- Religious and wisdom traditions: Buddhism, Christianity, Hinduism, Judaism, Islam, religion in general, ecospiritualists, indigenous elders, and an ancient-wisdom council with lineage advisors (for example, on how samsara, time and suffering relate). See [[Ecospiritualism]].
- Institutions and governance: military, professional organizations, nations, alliances, politics, business, economics, regulatory affairs, international affairs, the framers of the US Constitution and Declaration of Independence, historians, trial advocates, hackers, and a matrix leadership council.
- Science and systems: FMEA analysts, systemics, game theorists, evolutionary and developmental biology, mathematics, psychology, quality, futurists.
- Nature: ecology, and a council that speaks for the trees ("who speaks for the trees").

## Real-world examples
- Editorial panels in publishing and film that already review narrative realism.
- Institutional advisory boards that include faith and community voices.

## Why it matters to the book
- Each council tests a different part of human experience, which the book needs in order to judge whether characters ring true or feel uncanny.

## Open questions
- How many councils can run before the process becomes unmanageable?
- Which councils must be human, which may be AI, and which should be both?
- How are religious councils kept accurate and respectful?
