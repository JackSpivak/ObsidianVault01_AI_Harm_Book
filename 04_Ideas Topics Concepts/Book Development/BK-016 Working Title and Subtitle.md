# BK-016 Working Title and Subtitle

## Why this is distinct
- No note yet holds the book's working title. The [[Book_Brief]] has an empty "Working title" field, and the brief is read at the start of every session, so the title needs a place to be developed before it is entered there.
- "Flying under the radar" appears in [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]] only as a positioning strategy. This card treats the phrase as a possible title.

## Anticipated direction
- Test candidate titles against the book's core question and its stakes ([[BK-004 Stakes of the Inquiry]]).
- Pair each candidate title with a subtitle that states what the book does, and check both against the audience and voice in [[BK-003 Authorship, Voice and Style Guide]].
- Once the author chooses, enter the title in [[Book_Brief]].

## Conceptual territory
- Relates to the positioning strategy in [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]] ("Flying Under the Radar" and "in the gaps").
- Builds on the gap audit in [[BK-012 Questions Over Answers and the Book as Prompt]], which already uses "flying under the radar" and "flying in the gaps" as a test of what the book fails to open.

## Summary
- Candidate title, from the author's words: **Flying Under the Radar**.
- Candidate subtitle, from the author's words: **Flying in the Gaps**. The author suggested this as "possibly a title and subtitle thought".
- Both phrases describe a stance: the book works where attention does not reach, rather than against the institutions that would resist it. That fits the positioning strategy already in the vault, and it also fits the book's theme of harm that goes unnoticed.
- Open risk: "flying under the radar" can read as evasion. The book's central claims are about visible harms, so the title should not suggest that the book hides its position.
- there is a really interesting mirror issue here.  as humans in an AI dominated future we may very well need to fly under the radar for survival (or running between the legs of the dinosaurs to tie to past evolutionary survival imperitive)  but right now (evidence in the epic openAI/ hugging face attack of 2026) AI is also learning to "fly under the radar" of human oversight, detection and control

## Real-world examples
- Books whose title states their method rather than their topic.

## Why it matters to the book
- The title is the first thing a reader and a publisher see. It should state the book's stance, and this candidate does.

## Open questions
- Is "flying under the radar" the right title, or does it suggest hiding?
- Does the subtitle "Flying in the Gaps" need the title's first phrase to make sense?
- Should other titles be tested against the same stakes and audience?
- Who chooses the title: the author alone, or after testing with the councils?
