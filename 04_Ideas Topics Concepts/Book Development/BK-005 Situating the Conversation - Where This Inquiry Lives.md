# Situating the Conversation - Where This Inquiry Lives

## Why this is distinct
- It asks what situation the conversation lives in, and in which situations somebody believes it does not live when it actually does. It is about framing, not content.

## Anticipated direction
- Write a situation statement for the book and the councils: where the inquiry sits, who it includes, and what it leaves out.
- Keep a list of situations that people deny. These are the places where the inquiry is most likely to be blind.

## Conceptual territory
- Connects to [[Circumstance, Situation, Description and Networks]] (situation as a subset of circumstances) and to [[Ignorance, Blind Spots and the Naive Perspective]].

## Summary
- Every conversation has a situation: its participants, its tools, its time and its stakes. The book must state its own.
- Some people think a conversation does not live in a particular situation (for example, that AI harm is "not my problem", or that an ecological harm is outside the frame) when it actually does. Those denials are themselves data.
- The framing decides which questions are even askable.

## Original wording
> "The importance of framing or situating the conversation. Literally in what situation does this conversation live? And what situation does it not live or does somebody think it doesn't live when it actually does"

## Real-world examples
- A company that treats an AI incident as a PR matter rather than a safety matter, which frames the situation out of existence.
- A legal case framed narrowly so that wider harms fall outside the question.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book's claims depend on the frame it sets. A reader who disagrees with the frame will reject the claims for that reason alone.

## Open questions
- How do we record a situation without fixing it?
- Who has the authority to say a situation is in scope?
- Can the book's own situation be stated without ending up as one more frame?
