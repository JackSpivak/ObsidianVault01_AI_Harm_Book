# Doe v Apple Cobalt Child Labor Case

> [!warning] Partly verified 2026-11-09
> CNN blocked access (HTTP 451). I verified the items marked ✓ against ABC News (Emma Ogao, 6 Mar 2024) instead. The court opinion itself hasn't been read.

## Summary
Plaintiffs accused Apple, Alphabet (Google), Dell, Microsoft and Tesla over child labor in cobalt mines in the Democratic Republic of Congo. In March 2024 the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled the companies weren't liable.

## Key facts (with page/section)
- ✓ Defendants: Apple, Alphabet, Dell, Microsoft, Tesla.
- Suppliers named in coverage (Glencore, Umicore, Huayou Cobalt) were reportedly not defendants. (unverified)
- A federal district judge dismissed the case earlier, and plaintiffs appealed. (unverified)
- ✓ A U.S. appeals court ruled **3–0** that the companies couldn't be held liable. It found no more than an "ordinary buyer-seller transaction" with suppliers in the DRC.
- ✓ There were 16 plaintiffs, including former child miners and legal representatives of children who died or were seriously injured in the mines. ABC says the case was filed in **December 2022**. I believe it was filed in December 2019, so that date conflicts and needs checking.
- Plaintiffs' counsel, International Rights Advocates, called the ruling a setback. (unverified; ABC doesn't report it)
- I found nothing on later developments after March 2024.

## Useful quotes
All from ABC News, Emma Ogao, 6 Mar 2024, quoting the ruling or the complaint:
> "ordinary buyer-seller transaction" (the court)

> "would not bind the direct perpetrators of the unlawful labor, who are not before this court" (the court, on an injunction)

> "Many actors in addition to the cobalt suppliers perpetuate labor trafficking" (the court)

> "knowingly benefiting from and aiding and abetting the cruel and brutal use of young children" (the complaint)
