# Consideration: Defining Enough Care and Legal Precedent

## Core Statement / Thesis
Determining whether "enough care" has been taken requires examining legal standards, peer judgment, historical precedents, and the motivational roots of care.

## Elaborations & Webs of Thought
- **The Threshold of Sufficiency:** How will we know if enough care has been taken? Is there an endpoint? Historically, lovers, parents, soldiers, and philosophers have cared more than for their own lives.
- **Legal and Peer Standards:** In the law, sufficient care is judged by a jury of responsible, competent peers, guided by historical precedents and lighthouses pointing away from dangerous shoals.
- **Passion and Persuasion:** Care shares an original basis with passion—the key determiner of our ability to gather followers and affect the future. This points toward a core persuasive statement associated with each care that could help motivate remediators and prevent harm.

## Metaphors, Lenses & Imagery
- **Lighthouses as Precedent:** Historical precedents act as lighthouses built in the past, showing us dangerous shoals and paths to safety.

## Open Inquiries & Next Questions
1. How can a harm ontology incorporate a metric or qualitative standard for "sufficient care"?
2. What would a core persuasive statement look like for a specific harm category to motivate prevention?
