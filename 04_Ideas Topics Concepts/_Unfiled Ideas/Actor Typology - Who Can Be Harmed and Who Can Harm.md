# Actor Typology - Who Can Be Harmed and Who Can Harm

## Why this is distinct
- It sets out a working list of entity kinds (natural, legal, cyber, individual, collective, artificial, swarm, regulatory) that can act, be harmed, or both. It sorts them by role rather than only by biology or law.

## Anticipated direction
- Build a typology with two separate role relations: `assessedAs HarmfulActorEntity` and `hasSituationRole Actor`. An entity can be assessed as harmful without being the harmer, and can have a situation role without being an actor.
- Map each kind to what it can value, what it can be harmed through, and what it can answer for.

## Conceptual territory
- Feeds the actor and standing questions in [[Participation Threshold and Distance from Harm]] and [[HO-I.a Factor and CausalRole]].
- Overlaps with the agent-classes work in [[Harm Ontology - Explanations from 2026-11-19 (Agents and Instance Properties)]]. That note already separates causal capacity, purposive action and capacity to answer for an act. This card adds the entity kinds.

## Summary
- Entity kinds offered by the author:
  - Natural: individuals; flocks, herds, colonies, etc.
  - Legal: corporations, LLCs.
  - Cyber: bots, swarm agents, agent assemblies.
  - Collective: tribes, disciplines, organizations, armies, police forces, fire companies.
  - Regulatory: regulatory bodies, standards bodies.
- Some kinds may be both harmed and harmers. A herd can be harmed, and a corporation can both cause and suffer harm.
- Some kinds may need new categories, which fits the open-category thesis in [[Open Harm Categories - Harm Beyond Human Experience]].

## Real-world examples
- A police force as a collective actor that can both harm and be harmed by the public it serves.
- A corporation held to account for harm, and a standards body whose rules cause harm by omission.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book's escalating AI story moves from individuals to institutions to a coordinated AI whole. The typology is what lets the story represent that change in scale. See [[Scale Shift Twist - Institutions Versus a Coordinated AI Whole]].

## Open questions
- Is "swarm" a collective of bots, a single actor, or both?
- Should the regulator be a participant or a perspective?
- How do collective and individual kinds nest (a discipline made of individuals)?
