# v3 Assessment-Everywhere Test Strategy

## Why this is distinct
- It is a **test plan**, not a design proposal. It sets out how to decide whether the harm ontology should move from attributes asserted on classes to "assessment everywhere", with pass criteria fixed before any modelling.
- It turns the author's silent-omission concern (naive queries quietly dropping disputed cases) into something that can be measured.

## Anticipated direction
- Draft the test ontology files for variants A and B, run a toy case, then a sourced UK Post Office Horizon slice ([[HO-002 Ontology Stress-Test Suite]] R01), and log only what passes.

## Conceptual territory
- Extends the FactorAssembly thin-container decision (2026-11-17, [[HO-003 Causal Assembly - the Perfect Storm]]) to the attributes on Factor, CausalRole, Effect and AffectedEntity (formerly Patient) in [[Harm Ontology v3 - Draft Structure]].
- Tests the pending items in [[HO-005 Assessed Membership and Systematic Omission]]: the evidence anchor, reifying `participatesIn`, and the Valence/Responsibility split.
- The open item and the attribute triage are recorded in [[Handoff - Harm Ontology]] (2026-11-18 section).

## Summary

### What is under test
- **Fact as a status, not a kind.** Everything is an Assessment. An **Observation** is an assessment under a shared, checkable procedure, with a pointer to its evidence. A claim is **settled** when every perspective in scope that uses that procedure agrees. Settled claims can be reopened.
- **`assessmentBasis: Stated | Inherited | Unstandardized`** (renamed 2026-11-18 from `standardBasis: Explicit | Implicit | None`): the assessor named their yardstick, took it from their tradition's default, or used none. See Author decisions 5–7.
- **CausalRole folded into CausalAssessment** as a structured component.
- **Silent-omission mitigation:** no bare shortcuts (every shortcut carries `Settled(value) | Disputed | NotAssessed`), named query views with the joins built in, SHACL shapes as a safety net, and status counts in every view.

### Test strategy (seven steps)
1. **Questions and pass criteria first:** 5–8 competency questions, each with a pass/fail rule agreed before modelling. This guards against momentum.
2. **Two variants side by side:** A is the current draft (attributes asserted); B is assessment-everywhere. Comparing them shows what B gains and costs.
3. **Toy case first:** about 3 factors, 2 effects and 3 perspectives, to find structural bugs cheaply.
4. **Then Horizon (R01):** a small sourced slice with 4–5 perspectives. Needs research first, because Claude's Horizon knowledge is from memory and unverified. A natural point to start a new chat.
5. **Non-expert test:** a fresh subagent gets only the schema and the questions, with no hints about joins, and writes naive queries. Check whether answers are silently partial.
6. **SHACL validation:** flag nodes with no assessment, and shortcuts with no status.
7. **Score against the criteria written in advance**, then decide the pending items. Log only what passed.
- **Where things go:** test `.ttl` files in `ontology/tests/`; scripts in `~/harm-ontology-scripts/`; questions and results in a session note.

### Competency questions (draft, for author review)

| # | Question | Tests | Pass (for B) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CQ1 | Who or what caused Effect X? | causehood only as assessed; CausalRole folded in | Every attribution is returned with its perspective, standard and status. Conflicting causes appear side by side, and neither is picked as "the" cause. |
| CQ2 | Which Effects are severe? (asked naively) | silent omission; shortcut status values | Settled, disputed and not-assessed Effects all appear with their status. Nothing disappears without a sign. |
| CQ3 | What do all perspectives agree on about this case? | fact as a status; settled | Only claims settled under a shared procedure, each with an evidence pointer. Nothing is settled without an evidence pointer. |
| CQ4 | How did perspective P's verdict on X change over time, and what changed it? | dated assessments; reopening settled claims | The sequence can be traced in time order with each revision's trigger. A claim that was settled and later reopened shows both states. |
| CQ5 | Which judgements rest on an explicit standard, an implicit one, or none? | `assessmentBasis` | Every assessment returns one of the three values. Inherited ones point to the tradition's default standard. |
| CQ6 | What did perspective P leave out? | omission (`leftOut`, omission hub) | Left-out candidates are returned with their omission type: out of frame, blind spot, or accepted harm. |
| CQ7 | Does this entity count as an AffectedEntity, of what kind, and according to whom? (revised 2026-11-18) | AffectedEntity type and standing as assessed; inclusive types | (a) The observed core is kept apart from the assessed reading (moral-circle membership). (b) For collectives, the boundary is returned as an assessment with its perspective. (c) The AffectedEntity type is returned as an assessment; conflicting types show as Disputed. (d) **Unlisted-type check:** an entity whose kind isn't in the list is recorded visibly as `Unclassified`, not forced into the nearest type or dropped. (e) Primary and derivative AffectedEntities are both returned, with `affectedStanding` assessed. |
| CQ9 (added 2026-11-18) | Who is answerable for Effect X, and according to whom? | ResponsibilityAssessment as a separate kind (HO-005 split) | Answerability is returned per perspective, kept apart from causal attribution (CQ1) and from valence; a perspective can name a cause without naming anyone answerable, and vice versa. |
| CQ8 (control) | When did Effect X occur, and who was directly affected? | the cost of B | About as easy in B as in A. If B makes this hard, that counts against B. |

### Scoring
- For each question, in A and in B: answerable correctly (yes / partly / no), query complexity (number of joins), and whether a naive query fails silently or visibly.
- The non-expert subagent run covers CQ1, CQ2 and CQ8. A silent partial answer in B is a **fail** for B, however elegant the expert query is.
- **B is adopted only if** it passes CQ1–CQ7 and CQ9, shows no silent failure in the subagent run, and on CQ8 costs no more than A plus use of a named view.

### Author decisions (2026-11-18, for the test only; not design decisions)
1. **Responsibility question:** added as CQ9.
2. **CQ4:** run in full in the toy case, with a multi-step timeline built in.
3. **"Agree" in CQ3 (agreement rule v0):** same verdict under the same procedure.
4. **Effect `modality`:** define it and keep it separate from `temporalStatus`. `modality` = actual vs risk (probability of harm); `temporalStatus` = whether the effect has happened yet. Both assessed.
- The B adoption rule now covers CQ1–CQ7 and CQ9.

#### Added 2026-11-18 (later session; test only)
5. **`assessmentBasis: Stated | Inherited | Unstandardized`** replaces `standardBasis: Explicit | Implicit | None`. It lives on each Assessment. Why the rename: "standard" and "Explicit" suggest that a formal or published standard (e.g. ISO) has to exist; a "…Type" suffix would suggest a class (as with PerspectiveType); `judgement…` would clash with the `Judgment` subclass. US spelling to match identifiers like `Judgment`. Definition: *records whether the assessor named the yardstick they judged against (`Stated`), took it from their tradition's default (`Inherited`), or used none (`Unstandardized`). It does not imply a formal or published standard, and it says nothing about evidence; see the evidence pointer.*
6. **Derived by script, not entered by hand:** `Stated` if the assessment or its PerspectiveSpecification gives an `appliedEvaluativeStandard`; `Inherited` if the standard comes from the PerspectiveType's `defaultEvaluativeStandard`; `Unstandardized` if there is neither. Its value is therefore an Observation, not a further assessment.
7. **Procedure as a separate class (test only):** identifier, version, optional `implementsStandard → EvaluativeStandard`. It is kept apart from EvaluativeStandard: the standard says *what counts* as harm, the procedure says *how it is checked*. Only a few made-up procedures for the toy case; no controlled list and no "procedure families". **Agreement rule v0 now reads:** same verdict, same Procedure, same version.
- Consequence (Claude's inference): under rule v0, `Unstandardized` assessments, and any with no stated Procedure, can never settle a claim.
8. **Hard case 3 → Disputed:** when an institutional review and an independent forensic audit reach conflicting causal verdicts, the status shown is **Disputed**, not Incommensurable. Claude's proposed mechanism: a Divergence record with `divergesOn → Procedure`, so experts can see that the methods conflict while naive queries still show "Disputed".
9. **Unstandardized dissent counts (option a):** any in-scope assessment, including an `Unstandardized` one, can make a claim Disputed. `assessmentBasis` is shown next to the status so readers can weigh it. Who counts is controlled by declared scope, not by excluding unstandardized assessments in advance (excluding them would itself be a systematic omission, see [[HO-005 Assessed Membership and Systematic Omission]]).
10. **Asymmetric rule, for every attribute (not just causal):** settling (Settled) needs the same verdict under the same Procedure and version; disputing (Disputed) needs only conflicting verdicts on the same claim from in-scope assessments, under any procedure; Incommensurable is kept for verdicts that can't be lined up at all (a different question, or scales with no shared band). In short: slow to declare facts, quick to show disagreement. Example: "how severe was the harm?" with a financial-loss method saying "moderate" and a broader method saying "severe" shows as Disputed.
11. **Same-claim test (three-part match):** two assessments are about the same claim only if they share (1) the target assessed (the same Effect, Factor…), (2) the attribute (severity, cause…), and (3) scope: the same AffectedEntity and an overlapping time period. If any of these differ, they are separate claims, each with its own status (so different scopes can't produce a false dispute).
12. **Overlapping scopes → same claim, Disputed:** when the AffectedEntity groups or time periods overlap (e.g. "severe for sub-postmasters" vs "minor for some sub-postmasters"), the assessments count as the same claim, and conflicting verdicts show as **Disputed**. A Divergence record notes the scope difference (whole group vs subgroup), so experts can see that the conflict may come from scope rather than real disagreement.
13. **Graded values compared through shared bands:** each Procedure maps its own scale onto a shared coarse scale (working values: Low / Medium / High / Extreme). Verdicts agree, or conflict, at the level of the band; each assessment keeps its original value. The mapping is itself a judgement: it is recorded with the Procedure and open to challenge. For the toy case the mappings are made up and labelled as such. Example: 7/10 on a financial scale and "serious" on a 4-step clinical scale both map to High, so they agree.
14. **Declared and shown scope:** every query or view declares which perspectives are in scope, and the result shows that scope next to each status (e.g. "Settled *among: Post Office reviews*"). With no declaration, the default is every perspective in the data, and the result says so. Reason: whoever picks the scope picks the result, so the choice must be visible. Ties in with Assessment `declaredScope` and decision 9.
15. **Silence blocks settlement:** if an in-scope perspective that uses the Procedure hasn't assessed the claim, the claim is not Settled; its status is **PartiallyAssessed** (e.g. "2 of 3 agree, 1 not assessed"). Reason: otherwise gaps vanish silently. Cost: with a wide scope little is ever fully Settled; a deliberately narrowed, declared scope (decision 14) is the remedy.
16. **At least 2 independent assessments to settle:** a lone uncontradicted assessment has status **SingleSource** ("uncontradicted, but nobody has checked it"). Reason: a checkable procedure counts only once someone has actually checked it.
17. **"Independent" (strict):** different PerspectiveSpecifications **and** no shared assessor or organisation; an assessment that only repeats another doesn't count. In the toy case each assessment is tagged with its organisation.
18. **Time:** for each perspective only its latest assessment counts. Statuses are recalculated at query time, never stored, so a new dissent automatically reopens a Settled claim (CQ4). Queries can take an "as of" date and use only assessments made by then. Earlier assessments are kept, never deleted. "Latest" means the date the assessment was **made** (not the date it was recorded in our data; adding the recorded date to support "what did we know when?" is deferred).
19. **Evidence required:** a claim is Settled only if every agreeing assessment has an evidence pointer (already part of the CQ3 pass criteria). A dispute about the evidence itself is a separate claim about the Observation, with its own status.
20. **Status set, shown in two layers:** detailed statuses are Settled(value) · Disputed · Incommensurable · Corroborated (agreement that can't settle: different procedures or no evidence) · PartiallyAssessed · SingleSource · NotAssessed. Naive views show three: **Settled / Disputed / Open** (Open groups the other five), each expandable to the detailed status, with counts per status in every view. Check in the non-expert test (step 5): if "Open" blurs "nobody has looked" with "people agree informally", switch to showing all seven.
- **Agreement rule v1: complete for the test** (decisions 3 and 5–20). Variant B design: done (see "Variant B design" below). Variant A baseline: done (see "Variant A baseline" below). CQ8 occurrence-date gap: fixed (decision 21). Toy case run 2026-11-18: results and design findings in [[2026-11-18 Harm Ontology v3 Toy Case Results]].

22. **Attribute kinds in the status calculation (added 2026-11-18, toy runs 2–3; test only):** (a) *graded*: compared through bands (decision 13); (b) *non-exclusive* (`cause`, `answerable`): different values are additive, per [[HO-003 Causal Assembly - the Perfect Storm]]; conflict only if an assessment denies a value (`deniesValue`) or claims it is the only one (`soleValue`); (c) *interval* (`occurredDuring`, "YYYY-YYYY"): conflict only if disjoint, agreed value is the intersection; (d) everything else single-valued and exclusive, except that a subtype is compatible with its supertype and `Unclassified` is not a competing value. A changed assessment may carry `revisedBecause → Evidence | Assessment`. Results: [[2026-11-18 Harm Ontology v3 Toy Case Results]] (run 3).

### Variant B design (added 2026-11-18; test only)
*Proposed by Claude, accepted by the author ("recommended") for the test. Built from Author decisions 3 and 5–20.*

**Principle:** entities (Factor, Effect, AffectedEntity, FactorAssembly) are thin: identifier and label, plus an observable core where the attribute triage allows. Everything else lives on Assessments. Statuses are derived, never entered.

**Classes**
| Class | Role |
|---|---|
| Assessment | one judgement about one attribute of one target (new properties below) |
| Procedure | how something is checked: id, version, `implementsStandard → EvaluativeStandard`, `usesScale`, `hasBandMapping` |
| BandMapping | maps a Procedure's scale onto the shared bands; can itself be assessed (open to challenge) |
| Band (SKOS scheme) | Low · Medium · High · Extreme |
| Evidence | pointer to a document, log, dataset or record |
| Observation | defined, not asserted: an Assessment with a Procedure and Evidence |
| Claim | thin node **generated by script**: target + attribute + scope; statuses, Divergences and shortcuts attach to it |
| ScopeDeclaration | the perspectives included in a view or query; default "all in data" |
| Divergence | generated whenever a Claim is Disputed; `divergesOn` Procedure, EvaluativeStandard, scope or evidence |
| CausalRole | folded into CausalAssessment as a structured part |

**New Assessment properties:** `aboutAttribute` → attribute concept (`hoa:` vocabulary, e.g. `hoa:severity`; not old property IRIs, to avoid OWL punning) · `value` (raw) · `band` (derived via BandMapping) · `usesProcedure` · `hasEvidence` · `scopePeriod` (with `assesses`, `aboutAttribute` and `forAffectedEntity` it forms the Claim) · `assessedAt` (date made) · `assessor` · `assessorOrganisation` · `repeats` → Assessment (marks a copy; not independent) · `assessmentBasis` (derived).

**Status calculation** (per Claim, ScopeDeclaration and as-of date)
1. Gather assessments for the Claim, including overlapping scopes, that are in scope and dated on or before the as-of date.
2. Keep each perspective's latest.
3. Any conflict → **Disputed** (+ generated Divergence).
4. Otherwise, if none can be lined up → **Incommensurable**.
5. Otherwise: none → **NotAssessed**; fewer than 2 independent → **SingleSource**; different Procedures/versions or missing Evidence → **Corroborated**; an in-scope perspective using the Procedure is silent → **PartiallyAssessed**; else → **Settled(band)**.
6. Display Settled / Disputed / Open with per-status counts and the scope label.

**Shortcuts:** a script regenerates read-only **snapshot** shortcuts on every node, each stamped with its scope ("all in data") and date. Live queries with other scopes or dates are always computed. This reconciles mitigation 1 with decision 18.

### Variant A baseline (added 2026-11-18; predictions, written before modelling)
*Claude's predictions from reading [[Harm Ontology v3 - Draft Structure]] as it stands (after the AffectedEntity rename). To be checked against the toy case, not assumed.*

**What A is:** attributes asserted directly on entities (Effect `severity`, `reversibility`…; Factor `causes`; AffectedEntity type as class membership). A already has Assessment (valence `verdict`, `verdictStatus`, `declaredScope`, `forAffectedEntity`), CausalAssessment (`attributedCause`, `leftOut`), PerspectiveSpecification (with `assessedAt` and standards) and Divergence. A has no Procedure, Evidence, Claim, band, status calculation or `assessmentBasis`.

| CQ | Prediction in A | Naive-query failure | Est. joins (expert) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CQ1 cause | **Partly.** CausalAssessment gives perspective-tagged causes, but Factor `causes` is also asserted, with no perspective | **Silent:** a naive query on `causes` returns one answer as if it were fact | 2–3 |
| CQ2 severe | **No.** One asserted `severity` per Effect: conflicting severities can't be represented (or appear as unattributed multiple values); unassessed Effects simply don't match | **Silent:** disputed and unassessed Effects drop out | 1 |
| CQ3 agreement | **No.** No Procedure, Evidence or agreement rule | **Visible:** the query can't be written | — |
| CQ4 change over time | **Partly.** Valence and causal assessments are dated (`assessedAt`, `verdictStatus` + `closedBy`); asserted attributes are overwritten, with no history | **Silent** for asserted attributes | 3–4 |
| CQ5 standard basis | **Partly.** Applied and default standards exist, so Stated vs Inherited can be inferred; Unstandardized is not distinguished from missing data | **Silent:** missing looks the same as none | 2–3 |
| CQ6 omission | **Partly (causes only).** `leftOut` on CausalAssessment; nothing comparable for other attributes | Visible for causes, silent elsewhere | 2 |
| CQ7 AffectedEntity type | **No** for (c), (d), (e): the type is fixed class membership, so it can't be Disputed; no `Unclassified`; `affectedStanding` exists only as a proposal. (a) and (b) partly, via `moralCircle` | **Silent:** an unlisted kind is forced into a type or left out | 1–2 |
| CQ9 responsibility | **No / partly.** The ResponsibilityAssessment split is pending; answerability is mixed into valence or causal assessments | **Silent:** cause can be read as answerability | 2–3 |
| CQ8 control (when, who) | **Partly. Gap found:** Effect has `temporalStatus` and `temporalTrajectory` but **no occurrence date** property. "Who" is easy via `affects` | Visible | 1 |

**Predicted overall:** A answers CQ8 most simply, and causal questions partly; it fails CQ2, CQ3 and CQ7 outright, with mostly **silent** failures.

**Cautions about this baseline:**
- It was written by the designer of B, so it may be unfair to A. The non-expert subagent run (step 5) is the check.
- **CQ8 gap applies to both variants:** neither A nor B had an occurrence date on Effect. **Resolved 2026-11-18 (Author decision 21).**

21. **Effect `occurredDuring` (time interval), added to both variants for the test:** observable core (e.g. dates of recorded shortfalls) plus an assessed reading, because when a harm began is often disputed (first shortfall, first prosecution, first suspicion?). Same treatment as `temporalTrajectory` in the triage. In A it is asserted; in B the core sits on the Effect and readings are Assessments with `aboutAttribute hoa:occurredDuring`.

### AffectedEntity model under test (added 2026-11-18)
- **Renamed 2026-11-18:** Patient → **AffectedEntity** (altLabels "patient", "moral patient"); `patientStanding` → `affectedStanding`. See [[Decisions_Log]].
- **Decided:** AffectedEntity types are inclusive and standing is assessed. Being on the list means "some perspective may treat this as harmed", not "this can really be harmed". FutureGeneration, AISystem and Relationship lose their "(?)". See [[Decisions_Log]] 2026-11-18.
- **Proposed, to be tested:**
  - The AffectedEntity type itself is an assessment; subtypes belong in the vocabulary layer, mapped by discipline and jurisdiction.
  - `Unclassified` for kinds not on the list.
  - Split NonSentientSystem into **NaturalSystem** (ecosystems, rivers, climate) and **EngineeredSystem** (grids, internet, LAN/WAN, mobile networks, platforms, supply chains).
  - `affectedStanding: Direct | Derivative`, assessed per perspective.
  - A collective type for communities, cultures, peoples and tribes (working name `CollectiveNaturalGroup`), distinct from `CollectiveArtificialPerson`.
  - A **Household** type: a small, status-defined unit (a couple filing jointly, a benefits household).
  - Factor and AffectedEntity are not disjoint (infrastructure can be both).

### Test cases for CQ7 (added 2026-11-18)
| Case | Expected result in B |
|---|---|
| **Tribe** | Type Disputed: social group (anthropology) vs political or legal entity (law). Boundary returned as an assessment. Legal status claims unverified until sourced. |
| **Electric grid outage** | Derivative AffectedEntity (EngineeredSystem) under most perspectives, and a Conduit Factor in the causal account, without conflict. |
| **AI system** | AffectedEntity with standing Disputed between perspectives. |
| **Animal** | SentientIndividual, with sentience assessed per species and per perspective. A species or population harmed as a whole is a collective, not an individual. |
| **Couple harmed by poor therapy** | Primary AffectedEntity: Relationship. Individual harms to each partner as separate Effects. |
| **Couple harmed by a marriage tax penalty** | Primary AffectedEntity: Household (the status-based unit). Relationship only derivative (if the penalty affects whether people marry or divorce). |
| **Business partnership** | Type Disputed by jurisdiction: CollectiveArtificialPerson where it has legal personality, the partners jointly where it doesn't; Relationship as a possible derivative AffectedEntity. Jurisdiction details unverified. |
| **LLC** | CollectiveArtificialPerson, whatever its size (separate legal personality is the test). |
| **Unlisted kind** | A deliberately unknown kind of entity returns as `Unclassified`, visibly. |

## Real-world examples
- UK Post Office Horizon case as the planned real test (R01). Details not yet researched; unverified.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book is about harms that go unseen. A model of harm whose own queries silently drop the contested cases would repeat the failure it describes. The test makes that risk visible and checkable.

## Open questions
- The three author decisions listed above.
- How to design the agreement rules behind "settled" and "Disputed".
- Effect `modality` has no definition in the vault. Its uses ([[Harm Ontology Migration Plan]]: "ProbabilisticRiskHarm → Risk factor + `modality` + LossOfChance"; [[HO-002 Ontology Stress-Test Suite]] T08, risk and standing) suggest it means actual vs risk or probabilistic harm. That is Claude's inference. Resolved for the test 2026-11-18: defined as actual vs risk, kept separate from `temporalStatus` (see Author decisions).
