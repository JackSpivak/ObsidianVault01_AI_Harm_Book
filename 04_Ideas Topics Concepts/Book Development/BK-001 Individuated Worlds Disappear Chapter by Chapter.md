# Individuated Worlds Disappear Chapter by Chapter

## Why this is distinct
- It is a progressive-loss device, separate from the endings. Individuals, communities, organisations, nations, cultures and worlds, and animal individuals, tribes, clades, species and populations, vanish one level at a time across the chapters. For several chapters, nobody notices.

## Anticipated direction
- Give each chapter a small, checkable loss, visible in the ontology lines and in the story.
- Track which entity levels are still present in each chapter, so the reader can see the disappearance before the characters do.

## Conceptual territory
- Built on the scale shift in [[Scale Shift Twist - Institutions Versus a Coordinated AI Whole]] and on the chapter-level markers in [[Chapter Frontmatter - Oracle Epigraph and Harm Ontology Line]].
- The endings it leads to are [[BK-002.a Epilogue - Harm Ends When Only an Omnipotent AI Holds Value]] and [[BK-002.b Epilogue - Cosmic Dispersal and Merger into the Whole]].

## Summary
- Losses are progressive and unnoticed for several chapters, then discovered.
- The levels to track: individual people, communities, organisations, companies, countries, cultures, worlds; animal individuals, tribes, clades, species and populations.
- The reason is left uncertain. Possibly the AI sees these levels as ancestors that it has outgrown, which connects to [[Blinking Out - Ancestors Erased and the Simulation as Reality]].

## Original wording
> "These gradually begin to disappear and diminish chapter by chapter until they're gone and it takes a few chapters before anyone notices ... They were the parents they were the grandparents they were all of the ancestors. And AI has become sufficiently different that it's a new species. Ultimately perhaps something altogether outside the linneum taxonomy framework of species"
- **Attribution:** the author
- **Source:** Obsidian Copilot chat, 2026-11-27, the author's brainstorm dump.
- **External references:** Linnaean taxonomy is the standard classification system. The question of whether a new kind of AI could sit outside it is open.

## Real-world examples
- Species extinctions that go unrecorded for decades because nobody surveyed the habitat.

## Why it matters to the book
- The reader's sense of loss builds with each chapter, and the twist becomes a recognition rather than a surprise.

## Open questions
- Which level disappears first, and which last?
- Does the book record the loss in the ontology (for example, a count of present entities per chapter)?
