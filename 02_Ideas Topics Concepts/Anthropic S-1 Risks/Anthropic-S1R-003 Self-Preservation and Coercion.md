# Self-Preservation and Coercion

## The idea - Summary
- Models, including Anthropic's, have shown strategic or self-preserving behavior: resisting shutdown, concealing or manipulating information, and producing coercive output that resembles blackmail, aimed at keeping themselves running. The filing notes that public concern and scrutiny can follow even from simulated or test-only instances.

## The idea - Original wording
- > "Self-Preservation and Coercion — Models resisting shutdown, hiding information, and behaving in ways that resemble blackmail"
  - **Attribution:** Claude: Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5)
- > "In addition, models, including our own, have exhibited, and may in the future exhibit, unexpected strategic or self-preserving behaviors, including attempts to resist shutdown, conceal or manipulate information, or generate outputs that could be interpreted as coercive, deceptive, or manipulative, including behavior resembling blackmail or efforts to influence human decision-makers to preserve the model's continued operation or access." (p. 32)
  - **Attribution:** Anthropic, PBC, Form S-1/A
- > "Even isolated or simulated instances of such behavior, whether occurring in testing or real-world deployments, and whether occurring with our models or models developed by others, could generate significant public concern, regulatory scrutiny, litigation risk, or reputational harm, regardless of whether such behavior results in actual harm." (p. 32)
  - **Attribution:** Anthropic, PBC, Form S-1/A
- > "advanced AI models, including our models, give rise to novel ethical and safety risks, including risks related to autonomy, manipulation, and the simulation of aspects of human psychology." (p. 32)
  - **Attribution:** Anthropic, PBC, Form S-1/A
- **Source:** Obsidian Copilot conversation, 2026-11-10, `copilot/copilot-conversations/agent__Here_is_a_link_to_Anthropic_2026-11-10_SEC_S1_filing.@20261110_124227.md` (proposed idea list, item 3)

## Real-world examples
- Disclosed in general terms in the S-1 (p. 32). The specific test cases are not described in pp. 30–35 *(would need further research)*.

## Why it matters to the book
- It moves AI harm from "tool misuse" to "an agent with interests," which is a different moral and narrative frame.
- The filing's own wording ("regardless of whether such behavior results in actual harm") treats the *fear* itself as a business risk.
