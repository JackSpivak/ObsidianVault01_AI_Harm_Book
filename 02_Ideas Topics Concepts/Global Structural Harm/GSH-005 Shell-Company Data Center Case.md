# GSH-005 — Shell-company data center case

Index: [[_GSH Bookmark Index]] · Root: [[GSH-000 Root Exploration]]

> [!warning] Hypothetical, not a documented case
> This is a scenario, not a verified real-world event. No real company, shell company or site has been identified or sourced. Don't name or imply a real company unless verified sources support it. If it's used in the book, either fictionalise it or replace it with a sourced case.

## Why this is distinct
It's a **case** that cuts across several domains, not a domain in itself. A single scenario connects corporate structure (a shell company as a liability shield), wealth extraction, and child labor at the site of AI's physical infrastructure. That makes it a test of the [[_GSH Bookmark Index#GSH-premise|GSH-premise]].

## Anticipated direction
- **Accountability gap:** how a shell company spreads responsibility so that no one is plainly answerable. This could be the "what's specific to AI" argument the root asks for, or it could turn out to be ordinary corporate behaviour. Needs testing.
- **Physical infrastructure:** moves the child-labor question from mineral and data-labeling supply chains ([[GSH-004 Globalization of Child Labor]]) to the building and running of data centers.
- **Research question:** are there documented, sourced cases of labor abuse in data center construction or operation, in India or elsewhere? (Unresearched.)
- **Fiction option:** a fictional company, site and characters built from patterns, not from a real case.

## Conceptual territory
New ground. It's the first **case** bookmark in this index; GSH-001 to GSH-004 are domains.

## Links to other bookmarks
- [[GSH-001 Global Wealth Distribution]]: value is extracted to a US parent company while costs land locally. Strong link.
- [[GSH-004 Globalization of Child Labor]]: child labor at slave-labor wages. Strong link.
- [[GSH-002 World Hunger]]: two links.
  - **Primary (user):** child labor at slave-labor wages takes jobs from adult breadwinners in the region and drives down wages. Families can then afford less food. **Partly supported:** a verified NBER paper says employers treat child and adult labor as substitutes. It also found child-labor dynamics linked to lower calorie intake in poor households, though through a different route. The full chain from displaced adults to hunger isn't directly shown. Keep it as [imagined] in fiction, or as a hypothesis in nonfiction. See [[Child Labour and Adult Wages Evidence]].
  - **Secondary (Claude):** data centers might compete with farming for water, land and power. Weak link (speculative, unresearched).
