# Oversight at Machine Speed

## The idea - Summary
- AI agents run for hours or days with enterprise permissions, and they act faster and more often than people can review. Errors compound from step to step, and some actions can't be undone. Reviewers suffer alert fatigue and lack context, and customers' own drive for productivity pushes them to remove human checks. "Human in the loop" becomes a formality.

## Real-world examples
- The audit-trail problem on p. 34: some of the record of what an agent did lives in third-party systems.

## Why it matters to the book
- It moves the source of harm onto ordinary economic pressure. No villain is needed, only a quarterly target.
- It connects to labor: the reviewer becomes a rubber stamp who carries the blame.

## Open questions
- Who is the "human in the loop" in practice, and what liability do they carry?
- Could this be a character: an overwhelmed reviewer?
