# Regulatory Exemplars - Nuclear and Aviation

## Why this is distinct
- It asks which industries have regulated their hazards most successfully, and why. The nuclear and aviation industries are the proposed exemplars. The question is where mistakes and their consequences have been least.

## Anticipated direction
- Study nuclear and aviation regulation for the features that reduce errors: incident reporting, independent review, redundancy, and a culture that treats near misses as data.
- Compare them with AI governance and see which features transfer.

## Conceptual territory
- Links to [[Anthropic-S1R-004 Oversight at Machine Speed]] and [[002 Scalability of Oversight and Control]], which discuss oversight at speed. Relates to [[Method Toolkit - Vision, Purpose, Mission, Five Whys and FMEA]] (failure analysis methods).

## Summary
- Which regulators have the best record, and what do they do differently?
- Candidate features to test: mandatory incident reporting, a non-punitive reporting culture, independent inspectors, layered safety barriers, and public accountability.
- The question is whether any of these can be applied to AI without the same lag that the author flags in other cards.

## Real-world examples
- Civil aviation's incident reporting and crew-resource training.
- Nuclear plant safety regimes built on defence in depth.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book needs models of regulation that have worked, not only accounts of failure, so that its proposals are grounded.

## Open questions
- Which features of nuclear and aviation regulation depend on a small number of operators, and would not transfer to AI?
- Are the safety records comparable, and on what measure?
- What is the AI equivalent of a near miss?
