# 2026-11-21 Harm Ontology: Searchlight, Gap Identification and Method Session

> [!info] Status
> Session record. Nothing here is logged in [[Decisions_Log]] or filed in any bookmark index. Bookmark proposals are marked **proposed**. Lists are kept verbatim because some headings are candidate names for attribute groups in a revised ontology.

## Session intent
Test whether a discipline-based perspective can generate the questions, searchlight and harm findings that a harm ontology must represent, and whether the model can represent the search, its findings and its consequences or remedies. Along the way: a gap analysis of v3 against real-world assessment frameworks, a schema proposal, and a method description for a future skill.

## Chronology and purpose of each step

1. **Differentiating the perspectives (roster and competencies).** Purpose: think creatively about what a discipline-based perspective is for, and test whether the prompts can generate its narrative content. Value: made the perspectives comparable.
2. **Generating questions from one perspective.** Purpose: test whether one tradition yields enough distinct inquiries to act as a searchlight. Value: questions clustered into themes that can become attributes.
3. **Translating questions into observable harms.** Purpose: test whether a philosophical concept can be recognised in a news story or filing. Value: one question split into several checkable harms. This is the central test.
4. **Operationalising the judging layer.** Purpose: turn the premise (circumstances neutral, harm an assessment) into dimensions, instruments and a workflow. Value: shows what the ontology must record for reproducibility.
5. **Testing independence from the vault.** Purpose: a baseline without vault context. Value: separates what the vault adds from the general literature.
6. **Benchmarking against real frameworks.** Purpose: an external standard for judging the model. Value: judges the model against practice, not only its own terms.
7. **Gap-mapping v3 against the benchmark.** Value: located four gap groups (mandate, valuation, triggers, remedies). Core of the gap skill.
8. **Organising the gaps into a schema.** Value: camelCase classes and attributes that can be reviewed and tested. Visual depiction added at the author's request.
9. **Questioning the attachment point.** The author's challenge: the gaps hang off the wrong root and belong on a procedural layer, probably a `HarmAssessment`. Value: review caught an error earlier steps had missed. Shows the process is iterative.

Process note: the sequence is iterative, not linear. Step 9 revised step 7's placement.

## Method steps (proposed skill spine; placement test is part of the skill)

1. **Differentiating the perspectives.** Asking what each discipline-based perspective uniquely contributes made the perspectives comparable. Without this, every perspective looks like ethics under another name.
2. **Generating questions from one perspective.** Tested whether a single perspective yields enough distinct inquiries. First step of the searchlight.
3. **Translating questions into observable harms.** Central test: a concept is useful only if it can be recognised in a news story or filing.
4. **Operationalising the judging layer.** Dimensions, instruments and workflow; what must be recorded for reproducibility. Findings part of the searchlight.
5. **Testing independence from the vault.** Baseline; separates vault content from general literature.
6. **Benchmarking against real frameworks.** External standard, judging the model against practice.
7. **Gap-mapping v3 against the benchmark.** Mandate, valuation, triggers, remedies. Core of the gap skill.
8. **Organising the gaps into a schema.** camelCase classes and attributes, reviewable and testable.
9. **Questioning the attachment point.** Gaps placed on the wrong root; belong on a procedural layer. Why the placement test belongs in the skill.

Intention framing (proposed): This skill performs focused inquiry into the adequacy of a harm ontology. It starts from a discipline-based perspective, generates the questions it would ask (its searchlight, with its competencies and blind spots), and tests whether those questions yield checkable findings in real-world reporting. It then operationalises the judging layer, benchmarks it against institutional practice, and identifies gaps in how the model represents the search, the findings, and the consequences or remedies. The process is iterative. Success is measured by whether the model can represent a real assessment, not by the elegance of its internal structure.

Placement test (proposed, part of the skill): given a proposed element, ask whether it describes a tradition (PerspectiveType, default only), a particular act of assessment (HarmAssessment or Assessment), the thing assessed (Factor, Effect, AffectedEntity), or an output (verdict, remedy, closure). Split elements that fit several; record unclear ones as open placement questions.

## Lists generated (verbatim)

### Classical Council: 10 core questions (generated from the Classical perspective)
1. **The Character Formation Question (Aristotle):** *What kind of person does this system slowly train its users into becoming through daily, frictionless use?* (Focusing on dispositional drift rather than acute injury).
2. **The Faculty of Assent Question (Marcus Aurelius / Stoics):** *Does this tool protect or hollow out the user's capacity to withhold or grant mental assent, or does it automate their judgment away?*
3. **The Proper Excellence Question (Plato / Aristotle):** *Does this intervention make the human being better or worse with respect to their proper excellence and distinct function?*
4. **The Uncut Sprout Question (Mencius):** *Are we maintaining the structural conditions necessary for natural human capacities to sprout and grow, or are we engaged in repeated small cuttings that prevent regrowth?*
5. **The Relational Role Integrity Question (Confucian Ethics):** *Does the introduction of this machine or structure compromise the intrinsic integrity of fundamental human roles and relationships (e.g., parent-child, teacher-student)?*
6. **The Self-Harm of Wrongdoing Question (Socrates / Plato):** *In what ways does using or deploying this system corrupt the character of the actor or designer, independent of the external outcome on the recipient?*
7. **The Natural Flow vs. Artificial Force Question (Laozi / Taoism):** *Does this system operate through effortless alignment (wu-wei) with natural human dynamics, or does it impose rigid, brittle, over-engineered constraints that generate systemic brittleness?*
8. **The Hedonic Trap & Empty Desires Question (Epicurus):** *Does this structure manufacture artificial, insatiable desires and anxieties that disrupt peace of mind (ataraxia), or does it serve natural and necessary needs?*
9. **The Authority of the Inner Citadel Question (Marcus Aurelius):** *Where does the locus of control reside during a crisis: within the agent's reasoned reflection, or surrendered entirely to external system prompts and feedback loops?*
10. **The Cultivation vs. Extraction Question (Classical Synthesis):** *Does this environment treat human beings and communities as a renewable hill to be carefully tended, or as a barren resource to be grazed down to the bedrock?*

### Classical Council: 20 core questions (partitioned into four dimensions)

**I. Habituation & Character Formation (Aristotle)**
1. **The Dispositional Drift:** What underlying character traits or habits are quietly reinforced by the daily, frictionless mechanics of this system?
2. **The Passive User:** Does this technology train users to be passive recipients of answers rather than active cultivators of their own understanding?
3. **The Atrophy of Effort:** Which vital human capacities (such as memory, navigation, or critical friction) are atrophying due to automated substitutes?
4. **The Second Nature:** How does repeated interaction with this interface transform a user's spontaneous reflexes and moral intuitions over time?

**II. Internal Assent & Agency (Marcus Aurelius & Stoics)**
5. **The Hollowed Judgment:** Does this system prompt the user to reflexively click "approve" without ever engaging their capacity for genuine mental assent?
6. **The Inner Citadel:** Does the design protect the user's internal sanctuary of reflection, or does it continually breach their attention with external urgencies?
7. **The Locus of Control:** When a decision fails, does the user feel responsible for their own choice, or do they experience themselves as a mere cog in an algorithmic loop?
8. **The Illusion of Autonomy:** Are users given choices that feel like freedom while actually being funneled down pre-determined behavioral paths?

**III. Flourishing, Excellence, & Natural Order (Plato, Mencius, & Laozi)**
9. **The Proper Function:** Does this arrangement help a human being achieve their proper human excellence (arete), or does it degrade them to a mere instrument?
10. **The Ox Mountain Cuts:** What "repeated small cuttings" are occurring daily that prevent the natural sprouts of human development from ever taking root?
11. **The Artificial Constraint:** Does this system work in harmony with organic human rhythms, or does it impose brittle, hyper-rationalized rules that generate unseen friction?
12. **The Scale of the Soul:** Does this environment respect the natural scale of human community and comprehension, or does it overwhelm human cognitive and emotional limits?

**IV. Relational Integrity & The Harmer's Character (Confucius & Socrates)**
13. **The Proxy Relationship:** When an artificial entity stands in for one side of a human relationship, what essential moral texture of that bond is lost?
14. **The Corruption of the Maker:** What moral compromises does the creator or deployer of this system make within their own character in order to build it?
15. **The Distortion of Filial and Social Roles:** Does this technology strain or honor the mutual duties and reciprocity inherent in core human roles (parent, teacher, citizen)?
16. **The Unexamined Trade:** Are we trading deep, enduring relational bonds for shallow, frictionless efficiencies?
17. **The True Cost to the Actor:** If an unjust or dehumanizing system benefits the operator materially, how does it simultaneously impoverish their soul?
18. **The Art of Measured Proportion:** Does this practice observe the Golden Mean, or does it rush to technological extremes that imbalance the whole life?
19. **The Cultivated Garden:** Are we treating human communities as living ecosystems to be nurtured with patience, or as raw inputs to be optimized and harvested?
20. **The Ultimate Audit:** At the end of a lifetime shaped by this system, what kind of human being remains: one of measured wisdom, or one of hollowed-out compliance?

### Ten statements from one question (question 3, Proper Excellence / Uncut Sprout, reframed as assessable harms)
1. **Cognitive Atrophy (Skill Deprivation):** *Long-term reliance on automated workplace drafting tools generates a slow, cumulative decline in employees' independent writing, verification, and critical-thinking capacities, constituting a latent capability loss.*

3. **Institutional Deskilling:** *Systemic outsourcing of tactical decision-making to predictive algorithms over successive quarters erodes organizational memory and operational problem-solving competence across junior staff ranks.*

5. **Civic Competence Decay:** *Algorithmic curation that provides instant informational validation without cognitive friction creates an incremental decay in citizens' capacity for complex civic deliberation and nuance retention.*
6. **Epistemic Dependency:** *The structural design of personalized discovery loops trains users into passive epistemic deference, systematically impairing their autonomous capacity to evaluate raw evidence or seek out diverse perspectives.*
7. **Generational Knowledge Discontinuity:** *The rapid retirement of manual craftsmanship in favor of automated generative systems creates an irreversible break in intergenerational apprenticeship and tacit skill transmission.*
8. **Somatic and Physical Resilience Decay:** *Hyper-optimized delivery systems that minimize physical movement and spatial navigation contribute to a gradual atrophy of baseline spatial-orientation and physical self-regulation abilities.*
9. **Emotional Self-Regulation Erosion:** *Offloading emotional processing to frictionless companion algorithms over time impairs individuals' psychological capacity to tolerate distress, resolve interpersonal tension, or regulate affect independently.*
10. **Systemic Resilience Vulnerability:** *The widespread substitution of human heuristics with homogenous automated recommendations creates a systemic fragility, where populations lose the adaptive capacity to function when digital infrastructure experiences disruption.*

### Gap groups proposed for the schema (names are candidates for attribute groups)

Each group is attached to the wrong root in its current draft (`PerspectiveType`). The proposed attachment is a procedural layer, probably `HarmAssessment`. See the placement reasoning below.

1. **Institutional Mandate & Enforcement Authority.** Candidates: `statutoryBasis`, `enforcementPower`, `hasEnforcementAuthority`, `jurisdictionalScope`. Classes: `InstitutionalMandate`, `EnforcementAuthority`, `LegalStanding`.
2. **Methodological Metric Specification (Valuation & Currency).** Candidates: `valuationCurrency`, `permitsIncommensurableTradeoffs`, `aggregationFormula`. Classes: `ValuationMethodology`, `TradeoffRule`. Default-and-override pattern as for `defaultEvaluativeStandard`.
3. **Procedural Lifecycle & Trigger Rules.** Candidates: `mandatoryTrigger`, `triggerThreshold`, `assessmentTiming`, `deadlineDuration`. Classes: `AssessmentTrigger`, `ProceduralMilestone`.
4. **Remedial Closure & Prescriptive Output.** Candidates: `generatesRemedy`, `remedyEnforceability`, `mitigationRequirement`, `closureStatus`. Classes: `RemedialObligation`, `PrescriptiveOutput`.

Placement reasoning (summary): PerspectiveType is a tradition, with no statute, trigger, deadline or remedy of its own. An EIA is a lens used inside a legal regime, and the regime holds the veto. Mandate and trigger describe a procedure; remedies describe an output. Open: whether `EnforcementAuthority` should be an `Agent` (HO-007 concept); whether one remedy may be produced by one HarmAssessment and enforced by another body.
