# Harm-Opportunity Tension and Weighing

## Why this is distinct
- It names the oscillation between seeing everything as a problem and seeing everything as an opportunity, and proposes weighing harms and benefits against values to decide what to mitigate. It also raises the case where a severe harm to one person is minor at societal scale, unless that person was giving a major benefit.

## Anticipated direction
- Weigh harms and benefits against a stated value set, not on a single scale.
- Account for intensity: a low-intensity benefit and a high-intensity harm are not directly comparable without a common measure.
- Account for scale asymmetry: harm to one person may be large for them and small for society, unless that person's loss removes a major benefit from society.

## Summary
- If every harm is an opportunity and every opportunity a harm, the author asks how to decide what to do. The proposed answer is to weigh both against values.
- Intensity matters. A small benefit and a large harm can still be compared once both are measured against the same values.
- Scale matters too. A severe harm to an individual may be minor for society, unless that individual was providing a major benefit whose loss is large.
- The optimism-pessimism split may be a perspective, not a discipline, and should be treated as an assessor trait ([[Assessor Situation and Standpoint]]).

## Real-world examples
- A pharmaceutical with serious side effects that saves many lives. Weighing needs both intensity and scale.
- A whistleblower whose disclosure harms a firm but protects the public.

## Why it matters to the book
- Without a weighing rule, the book cannot say what to mitigate. This card supplies the decision layer that the harm ontology lacks.

## Open questions
- What common scale allows intensity of harm and benefit to be compared?
- Is a benefit to a person's role (for example, a surgeon) a benefit to society, and does it transfer when the person is lost?
- Who holds the weighing authority, and does it sit with a council or an AI?
