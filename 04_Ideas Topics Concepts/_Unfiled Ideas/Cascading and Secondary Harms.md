# Cascading and Secondary Harms

## Why this is distinct
- It revisits the cascade concept in [[HO-I.b Effect and Cascades]] and asks for an explicit class of follow-on harms: harms that arise from an earlier harm, rather than directly from the act.

## Anticipated direction
- Add a secondary-harm relation, linking a primary harm to the harms that follow from it.
- Record the chain's length, its branching and the point at which a cascade becomes independent of its origin.

## Conceptual territory
- Revisits [[HO-I.b Effect and Cascades]]. It relates to [[HO-006 Causal Chain - an Assessed Selection with a Horizon]] (the chain is the path, the cascade is what it produces).

## Summary
- Harms can cascade: a lost job leads to missed rent, eviction, and then ill health.
- A follow-on harm may be more serious than the primary harm, and may appear long after it.
- The ontology must say where a cascade ends, which depends on horizon and on participation ([[Participation Threshold and Distance from Harm]]).

## Real-world examples
- A data breach that leads to identity theft and then to loss of credit access.
- A factory closure that causes family breakdown and later mental-health harm in the community.

## Why it matters to the book
- The story's personal harms become institutional harms through cascades. Showing the cascade is how the book shows the scale shift.

## Open questions
- Is a secondary harm a new harm or the same harm at a later stage?
- How far down the cascade is the original perpetrator still responsible?
- Can cascades be modelled as the temporal trajectories described in the gap assessment ([[design-note-harm-ontology-v0.03.01-gap-assessment]])?
