**ai**: Your question makes sense. The answer is that there are two separate mechanisms, and they are often confused.

**Namespaces vs imports**
- **Namespaces** (`ho:`, `hov:`, `law:`) only name things. They are IRI prefixes, and they load nothing.
- **`owl:imports`** is what loads files. A file that imports another brings that file's contents into the same workspace.

**How the three layers would be tied together.** Each layer would be its own small ontology file with its own ontology IRI and version IRI. A fourth, tiny file imports the other three:
- `ho-core.ttl`: the OWL structure. It imports nothing from the other layers, so it works on its own.
- `ho-profiles.ttl`: the SHACL shapes. It imports the core.
- `ho-vocabulary.ttl`: the hubs and discipline schemes. It imports the core, so the IRIs it points to resolve.
- `ho-all.ttl`: it only says "import the three above".

**In Protégé you'd open `ho-all.ttl`.** The imports load automatically, and the Active Ontology tab lists the import closure. You don't load the three files by hand. If Protégé can't fetch an import from the web, it resolves it from local files, using the folder or a `catalog-v001.xml` file. That's from my general knowledge of Protégé, so check it against your version.

**What you'd see**
- **Core classes and properties:** in the Classes and properties tabs, with their short definition, altLabels, `designStatus` and source in the Annotations pane. This is where option 1 pays off: the class is readable on its own.
- **Hubs and spokes:** these are `skos:Concept` instances, so they appear in the **Individuals** tab, not the class tree. Their definitions, `closeMatch` links and discipline-scheme membership show in the description pane.

**The general fact: in OWL, properties are not attached to classes, and nothing is "inherited".**
- **Domain and range** are axioms about how a property may be used. If you assert `x p y` and `p` has domain `C`, a reasoner infers that `x` is a `C`. It does not make `p` a field of `C`.
- **"only" restrictions** (what I put on `PerspectiveType`) describe what values a link may have. They don't create entry fields either.
- **Any individual can carry any property assertion.** Nothing is pre-listed for it. Annotation properties work the same way.
- **What instances do inherit:** their class memberships, and, through sub-properties, the super-properties. So the group tree works for display, but not as a form.

That Protégé Desktop has no instance entry form is from my general knowledge, so please correct me if your version differs. The "properties of this class" list you may expect comes from older frame-based editors, not OWL editors.

**How to get the right properties in front of someone entering an Assessment**
1. **Give every class its properties.** Set the domain from the draft structure's `props:` lines for Assessment, CausalAssessment, Effect, Factor and the rest, not only for PerspectiveType. You'd then find them by selecting the class and looking at its usage list. I believe that list includes the domain axioms. Nothing prompts you, though.
2. **State the expected properties as restrictions.** For example `Assessment SubClassOf (assesses some …)`. It documents what a typical Assessment has, and it's visible on the class page. Under open-world semantics it doesn't force anything to exist.
3. **Use SHACL profile shapes.** This is the standard way to say what properties instances of a class have, whether required or optional, in what order and group. That's the layer 2 job from your 2026-11-17 decision. Form-generating tools such as TopBraid read these shapes. I don't know of a native Protégé form built from them.
4. **Pre-filled template individuals.** You'd create a template Assessment with the expected properties and duplicate it. I believe Protégé has a "duplicate individual" command, but I haven't verified it.
5. **Spreadsheet entry.** The Cellfie plugin, if your version has it, maps spreadsheet columns to property assertions, which would act as a form. I haven't verified it either.

**1. Does agency require sentience?** English uses "agent" in at least three senses, and they pull apart:

- **Legal agent:** someone who acts on another's behalf (agent and principal).
- **Causal agent:** anything that brings about an effect, as in "agent of erosion" or "chemical agent". Nothing intentional is needed.
- **Linguistics** (from memory, unverified) separates the doer ("agent", usually animate) from a natural "force" or "instrument". It says "the storm destroyed the roof" with a force, not an agent. It's the same field that makes "patient" a neutral undergoer, which your Decisions_Log already notes.

So sentience isn't required in the loose, causal sense, but the strict sense needs more. The model should say which sense it means and not inherit the ambiguity.
