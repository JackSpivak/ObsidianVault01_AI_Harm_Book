# Mirror Roles - Rescuer, Mitigator, Beneficiary and Intermediary

## Why this is distinct
- It proposes a set of mirror roles for each harm role. For every role in harm (object, means, perpetrator), there is a counterpart on the rescue and benefit side (rescuer, mitigator, beneficiary), with intermediaries in between. The author is unsure whether the mirror is for the rescuer or the beneficiary and intermediary, and this card keeps both.

## Anticipated direction
- Add mirror roles to the ontology: rescuer and mitigator for the harm side, beneficiary and intermediary for the gain side.
- Give each harm role a mirror, so that the ontology can describe the same event from both sides.

## Conceptual territory
- The benefit side already has a mirror structure in the "Benefit Mirror" in [[Interdisciplinary_Harm_Ontology_Compendium v2a]] (`harm:ValueCapture` and the B14–B23 mirrored attributes). This card adds the role names and the intermediary.
- Pairs with [[Object Properties and Annotation Properties]].

## Summary
- Harm roles: object of harm, means of harm, perpetrator, victim.
- Mirror roles: means of rescue, rescuer or mitigator, beneficiary, and intermediary (someone who carries the harm or gain between parties).
- An intermediary can be both a conduit and a beneficiary, so the roles must be separate attributes, not one role.

## Real-world examples
- A charity (rescuer) that funds a clinic, with the clinic as intermediary and patients as beneficiaries.
- A payment processor (intermediary) that carries money from a fraud to its beneficiary.

## Why it matters to the book
- The story will have rescuers and beneficiaries as well as perpetrators, and the ontology must be able to describe them.

## Open questions
- Is the mirror for the rescuer or for the beneficiary, as the author was unsure?
- Can one agent hold several roles at once without the ontology double-counting?
