# HO-premise — No Intrinsic Harmfulness

Index: [[_Harm Ontology Bookmark Index]] · Premise

## Why this is distinct
- This is the assumption the model depends on most heavily: nothing (no factor, role or effect) is harmful or beneficial in itself. Valence exists only in an assessment, indexed to patient, standard, horizon and scope. If it's false, HO-I.b, HO-II.a, HO-II.b and HO-III.a lose much of their force.

## Anticipated direction
- Test it against hard cases. Are there harms so severe that every standard and every horizon agrees (torture, death)? If so, does universal agreement mean intrinsic harmfulness, or just a convergence of assessments?

## Conceptual territory
- New. It resembles relational accounts of value, but it's stated here as a modelling rule rather than a metaethical thesis.

## Summary
- Roles and effects can be harmful or helpful depending on circumstance. An effect can itself become a beneficial factor further along the chain. So the ontology never labels anything harmful outright; verdicts are Harmful / Beneficial / Mixed / Neutral / Contested, always qualified by perspective.

## Original wording
> "just as a given role may be harmful or helpful depending on the circumstance, so can be an effect. especially if the state induced in the obeject of harm itself becomes a beneficial agent in a causation chain."
- **Attribution:** Author

> "It means that **harmful vs. beneficial isn't a built-in property of anything in the model**: not of factors, roles or effects."
- **Attribution:** Claude: Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5)

- **Source:** Obsidian Copilot agent session "philosopher adverse stress test harm ontology", 2026-11-14; [[2026-11-14 Harm Ontology Redesign]].
- **External references:** none.

## Real-world examples
- Pain from an injury leads to treatment, and a serious illness is caught. A public AI failure leads to new safety law. In the other direction, convenience becomes dependence, which becomes deskilling.

## Why it matters to the book
- The AI industry's main defence ("net benefit") is an offsetting argument. This premise forces every verdict to declare whose good, over what span, and under what standard.

## Open questions
- Is "Contested" a verdict, or a sign that a Divergence needs to be recorded?
- Does the rights-based standard break the premise, since a wrong may be wrong whatever the outcome?
