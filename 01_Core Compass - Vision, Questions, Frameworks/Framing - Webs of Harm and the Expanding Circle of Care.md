# Framing: Webs of Harm and the Expanding Circle of Care

## Core Statement / Thesis
Every harm involves a setback to a valued situation, person, object, consideration, right, or privilege, unfolding across interconnected webs of causes and outcomes rather than isolated events.

## Elaborations & Webs of Thought
- **Anatomy of a Setback:** Understanding a harm requires knowing how it happened, what elements were involved, why it occurred, its consequences, and whether it represents an inevitable path toward worse harms or a recurring risk exposure.
- **Webs vs. Chains:** Harms, their sources, and their outcomes form dense webs in which we and those we love become entangled.
- **Responsibility and Remediation:** A responsible assessment invites and requires structured inquiry to discover un-noticed harms, account for them, take responsibility, make amends through proper remediation, and establish future safeguards.

## Metaphors, Lenses & Imagery
- **Perspectives as Searchlights:** Perspectives illuminate specific angles of a situation.
- **Care as a Lighthouse:** Care acts as a guardian and guide, revealing dangerous shoals and paths to safety. 
- **Enlightenment and Wonder:** Wondering is the first step of enlightenment, bridging observation with care.

## Open Inquiries & Next Questions
1. How do we map the boundary of a harm web before it expands uncontrollably?
2. How can automated or structured inquiries trace future trajectories of harm without falling into speculative noise?
