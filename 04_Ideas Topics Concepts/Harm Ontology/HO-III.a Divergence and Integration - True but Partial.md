# HO-III.a — Divergence and Integration: True but Partial

Index: [[_Harm Ontology Bookmark Index]] · Parent: [[HO-000 Harm as Perspective-Relative Assessment|HO-000]] · Tier HO-III (meta layer)

## Why this is distinct
- This is the ontology's main value-add. Existing ontologies assert harm. This one **represents, explains and names disagreement** about harm, as a first-class `Divergence` object.

## Anticipated direction
- `Divergence`: between (Assessments), divergesOn (any Perspective property), divergenceType, reconcilable.
- Types: **Perspectival** (different parts of the elephant; both can be true), **Normative** (different standards), **Evidential** (different information), **Factual** (contradictory claims about the same thing; at least one is wrong).
- Optional `Integration`: combines partial Assessments and records what it kept in frame and what it left out.

## Conceptual territory
- New. It draws on Wilber's "true but partial" (from memory, unverified) and the blind men and the elephant.

## Summary
- Guardrail against relativism: truth is relative to a perspective, but a perspective's *value* is assessed, not assumed, through fitness, coverage, missingness and assessor independence. An industry impact assessment can be true but partial *and* rank low on coverage and independence. The Factual type keeps "partial" from becoming an excuse for "false".

## Real-world examples
- Sai Weng: the neighbours vs. the old man (Perspectival, on horizon); the household vs. other families (Perspectival, on framing); the household vs. the state (Normative, on standard).

## Why it matters to the book
- Most real harm disputes mix perspectival and factual disagreement, and the parties rarely know which kind they're having. Naming it is a contribution in itself. Courts, councils and the book all perform Integrations.
