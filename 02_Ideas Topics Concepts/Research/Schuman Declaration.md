# Schuman Declaration

## Summary
- On 9 May 1950, French foreign minister Robert Schuman proposed pooling French and German coal and steel production under a European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC).
- The explicit goal was to make war between France and Germany "not merely unthinkable, but materially impossible."
- The ECSC was the first supranational European institution and led eventually to today's EU.
- It's an example of [[Peace Can Be Built Intentionally|peace built intentionally]] by structural design: shared resources as a peace engine.

## Key facts (with page/section)
- **9 May 1950:** declaration presented by Robert Schuman, French foreign minister. (EU history page)
- **Proposal:** a European Coal and Steel Community whose members would pool coal and steel production. (EU history page)
- **Outcome:** the ECSC had six founding members, including France and West Germany, and "was the first of a series of supranational European institutions" leading to today's EU. (EU history page)
- **Treaty:** the ECSC treaty was the Treaty of Paris, signed in 1951. *(unverified)*: not stated on the EU page checked

## Useful quotes
> "any war between France and Germany becomes not merely unthinkable, but materially impossible."
> (Schuman Declaration, via EU history page and Fondation Robert Schuman)

## My interpretation
- **Interpretation (Claude):** France and Germany fought three wars between 1870 and 1945 and none since. Crediting the ECSC alone would overstate it; NATO, the Cold War and US influence also mattered.
- **Interpretation (Claude):** this fits the construction analogy. Pooled coal and steel became a load-bearing wall, and the stated design intent was to make war physically hard, not just undesirable.
