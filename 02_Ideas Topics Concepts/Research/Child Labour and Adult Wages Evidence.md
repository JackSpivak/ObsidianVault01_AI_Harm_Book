# Child Labour and Adult Wages Evidence

> [!warning] Unverified
> These notes come from web search summaries. The papers weren't opened. The author names are from memory and need checking.

## Summary
This note tests the user's proposed mechanism for [[GSH-005 Shell-Company Data Center Case]]: that child labor pushes down adults' wages and jobs, so families can afford less food. **I found no strong causal study supporting it.** The better evidence points the other way, or is mixed.

## Key facts (with page/section)
- After India's 1986 child-labor ban, children's wages fell about 4% relative to adults', with the biggest fall in manufacturing. Children moved into other kinds of work instead of leaving work. (NBER w19602; VoxEU)
- Higher minimum wages for adults reduced child work in domestic chores and household businesses, and adult employment rose in rural areas. (ScienceDirect, *Child labor and the minimum wage: Evidence from India*)
- Older case studies report children paid about 60% of the adult wage for the same work, for example in Firozabad's glass-bangle industry. (Small, dated studies)
- A 2007 review by Edmonds reportedly found no study of work restrictions that met current standards of evidence.
- A state-level study reportedly found that child and adult labor rates rose together, not against each other.
