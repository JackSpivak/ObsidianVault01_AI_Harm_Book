# Chapter Frontmatter - Oracle Epigraph and Harm Ontology Line

## Why this is distinct
- It's a structural, literary device: a repeating two-part block at the head of each chapter. It's not a theme or a case of harm. It joins the book's narrative voice to the harm ontology.

## Anticipated direction
- Define the sage/AI voice and how it is revealed. Fix a machine-readable format for the identifier lines (2a premise harms, 2b emergent harms), possibly with a Turtle statement or IRI for each story, so curated stories could become teachable harm instances.

## Conceptual territory
- New as a craft device. It touches ontology territory already covered in [[003 Harm Ontology from Outlines]] and [[Interdisciplinary_Harm_Ontology_Compendium]], here approached as a way of making the book itself machine-readable.

## Summary
- Each chapter opens with a standard epigraph/heading block containing two entries:
  1. **Quote, insight or "seeing"** about the current state of the world, spoken by an invented but wise being. The voice can't tell objective reality apart from the growing hybrid of real and synthesized reality. The being may turn out to be the AI itself. One possibility: a Lao Tzu–like figure who "hallucinates" a stream of consciousness drawn from evolving `worldscontext.md` documents.
  2. ** Emergent harms (possible):** a slightly different line for harms identified *after* the story unfolded.
  3. 
  
  3. **Harm ontology identifier line(s)**: technical identifiers, including namespace entries, for harm categories and specific harm types. It should appear as cryptic essentially machine readable code pointing to the actual harm ontology.  Possible also a .ttl (turtle) statement or IRI for the story as a whole - so that (after vetting/curation) these imaginative stories could be actual learnable/teachable instances of harm - possibly to be absorbed into the Harm Ontology
     - **2a. Premise harms:** the harms identified in the story premise and used as the basis for story synthesis.

## Why it matters to the book
- The epigraph voice blurs real and synthesized reality and may be revealed as the AI, so the structure itself carries the book's theme.
- The ontology line makes the harm taxonomy visible. The "before" and "after" lines can show the gap between the harms that were planned and the harms that emerged.
