Ancient Greek and Eastern (Buddhist, Taoist, and Confucian) philosophies map this exact causal chain using deeply intentional nouns, though they tell fundamentally different stories about **where harm originates**.

While ancient Greek thought frames harm as a **chain of character, error, and tragic necessity**, Eastern traditions frame it as **ignorance, relational disharmony, or obstruction of natural flow**.

---

1\. Ancient Greek Philosophy (Aristotelian & Tragic Causation)

In Greek tragedy, ethics, and jurisprudence, harm is traced from an internal flaw in judgment to an external act of transgression, culminating in cosmic or civic ruin.

* **Hamartia (Error / Missing the Mark)** *(Harmful Condition / Latent Flaw)* — *"The actor possesses a blind spot, ignorance, or defect in judgment before any crime occurs."*  
* **Prohairesis / Boulēsis (Deliberate Choice / Wish)** *(Harmful Intent)* — *"In Aristotle’s ethics, the rational desire or corrupted will that directs an actor toward an unjust end."*  
* **Hubris (Overreach / Arrogant Outrage)** *(Harmful Actor \+ Disposition)* — *"The inflated pride that emboldens an actor to violate mortal limits or the dignity of others."*  
* **Adikia / Atē (Wrongdoing / Blind Folly)** *(Harmful Action)* — *"The specific unjust act (Adikia) or reckless, deluded strike (Atē) that sets destruction in motion."*  
* **Peripeteia (Reversal of Fortune)** *(Harmful Event)* — *"The sudden, decisive turning point where the action triggers an irreversible collapse."*  
* **Miasma (Pollution / Stain)** *(Harmful Situation)* — *"A lingering state of moral and civic contagion that infects the household or city until purified."*  
* **Nemesis / Pathos (Retribution / Suffering)** *(Harmful Outcome / Target)* — *"The inevitable downfall, grief, or cosmic correction experienced by the target and perpetrator alike."*

---

2\. Buddhist Philosophy (The Chain of Dependent Origination & Karma)

Buddhism (*Pratītyasamutpāda*) views harm not as a linear strike from an external villain, but as an **unbroken, self-reinforcing cycle of psychological delusion and suffering**.

* **Avidyā (Ignorance / Delusion)** *(Harmful Condition)* — *"The root blindness to the true, interconnected nature of reality."*  
* **Kleśas (Mental Afflictions / Three Poisons)** *(Harmful Intent)* — *"Greed (Rāga), hatred (Dveṣa), and delusion (Moha) arising in the mind."*  
* **Cetanā (Volition / Intention)** *(Harmful Actor's Will)* — *"The conscious impulse that transforms a passing thought into moral action."*  
* **Akuśala-karma (Unwholesome Action)** *(Harmful Action)* — *"The specific destructive deed committed through body, speech, or mind."*  
* **Sparśa & Vedanā (Contact & Sensation)** *(Harmful Vector \+ Event)* — *"The collision of action with a sense object or target, triggering an immediate painful or reactive sensation."*  
* **Saṃsāra / Upādāna (Entanglement / Clinging)** *(Harmful Situation)* — *"The ongoing trap of reactivity and cycle of compounding harm."*  
* **Dukkha / Vipāka (Suffering / Karmic Ripening)** *(Harmful Outcome)* — *"The inevitable distress, alienation, and painful consequence borne by beings."*

---

3\. Classical Chinese Philosophy (Confucian & Taoist Framings)

Chinese traditions split into two complementary lenses: **Confucianism** frames harm as a breakdown of social order and ritual, while **Taoism** frames it as forceful interference with nature.

**Confucianism (Relational & Civic Disharmony)**

* **Bù Rén (Inhumanity / Callousness)** *(Harmful Condition)* — *"An atrophy of empathy and moral cultivation within the person or ruler."*  
* **Sī Yù (Selfish Desire)** *(Harmful Intent)* — *"Putting private gain above communal duty and righteousness (Yì)."*  
* **Shī Lǐ (Breach of Ritual / Propriety)** *(Harmful Action)* — *"Violating the social norms and respectful boundaries that hold relationships together."*  
* **Luàn (Chaos / Disorder)** *(Harmful Event \+ Situation)* — *"The breakdown of hierarchy, trust, and social stability."*  
* **Huò (Calamity / Misfortune)** *(Harmful Outcome)* — *"Widespread suffering, rebellion, or ruin of the state and family."*

**Taoism (Coercion & Obstruction)**

* **Wàng (Deluded Ambition / Recklessness)** *(Harmful Intent)* — *"Trying to control, hoard, or force outcomes against the natural grain."*  
* **Yǒu Wéi (Coercive / Artificial Action)** *(Harmful Action)* — *"Meddling, aggressive intervention that disrupts organic balance."*  
* **Nì (Going Against the Current)** *(Harmful Vector)* — *"Friction created by opposing the natural way (Tao)."*  
* **Zāi / È (Disaster / Obstruction)** *(Harmful Event \+ Situation)* — *"A blockage in flow that builds pressure until the system ruptures."*  
* **Wáng (Loss / Exhaustion)** *(Harmful Outcome)* — *"Depletion of vitality, premature collapse, and ruin."*
