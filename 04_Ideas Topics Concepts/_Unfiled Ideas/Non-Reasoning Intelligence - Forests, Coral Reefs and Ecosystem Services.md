# Non-Reasoning Intelligence - Forests, Coral Reefs and Ecosystem Services

## Why this is distinct
- It asks whether something that does not reason can still be intelligent, using forests and coral reefs as cases. It also asks which questions can be answered without arguing about human intelligence at all, and what value a forest provides, using the discipline of ecosystem services.

## Anticipated direction
- Define intelligence in a way that does not require reasoning, for example as adaptive, self-regulating or informative response.
- Use ecosystem services (provisioning, regulating, cultural, supporting) as a vocabulary for what natural systems do for harm and benefit.
- Ask questions a forest or reef would answer, and treat ecological systems as assessors with their own values.

## Conceptual territory
- Relates to [[Ecospiritualism]] and to the "Council of Ecology" and "Council that speaks for the trees" in [[Council Roster Expansion]].
- Links to the benefit side of the ontology in [[Harm-Opportunity Tension and Weighing]].

## Summary
- Is it possible for something that does not reason to be intelligent? A forest adapts, distributes resources and recovers from damage.
- Ecosystem services are a framework for measuring what nature provides, and so for seeing harm to those provisions.
- Questions answered by a forest avoid arguing over human intelligence and so can be asked without that dispute.
- Divine or designed agency (whether some agency could be designed to eliminate harm) is raised here as a related question, and needs its own card if pursued.

## Real-world examples
- Mangrove protection against storm surge, valued as a regulating service.
- Coral reefs as fisheries and as coastal protection, with harm measured against both.

## Why it matters to the book
- It widens the book's notion of intelligence and assessor beyond humans and machines, which the book's harm ontology needs if it is to be complete.

## Open questions
- What makes a non-reasoning system an assessor, if it is one at all?
- Does the ecosystem-services framing treat nature as a resource, against the spirit of the ecospiritualist view?
- Is "divine designed agency" a metaphor the book can use, or a claim it must defend?
