# What Does Love Intend - Council of Love

## Why this is distinct
- It asks what love intends, citing Rumi, and proposes a Council of Love. It gives the book a perspective in which the test of harm is what an act intends for the other, not only what it does.

## Anticipated direction
- Use "what does love intend?" as a diagnostic question on each harm case: did the act aim at the other's flourishing or at its own gain?
- Let the Council of Love and the Council of Happiness test this question against the cases.

## Conceptual territory
- Connects to intention in [[Dreams and Mischief - Boundary Tests of Harm]] and to the Council of Happiness in [[Council Roster Expansion]].

## Summary
- Love is a perspective with its own values and intentions. An act that causes pain can still be an act of love, and an act that causes no visible harm can still betray.
- The question tests whether an assessment looks at intention as well as effect.
- A risk to name: this may be read as sentimental. The council should be asked to test it, not only to affirm it.

## Real-world examples
- A parent who forbids a teenager's choice out of love and causes a rift, against one who forbids it out of control.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book's harm test needs a way to separate harm done with care from harm done with indifference or malice.

## Open questions
- Can intention be assessed independently of what it produces?
- Does the Council of Love have a different standard for harm, or the same standard with different questions?
