Extending the pattern of **a harmful actor, a harmful action, a harmful event, a harmful situation** follows a clear progression: **it moves from who causes harm, to what they do, to what happens as a result, to the broader context or conditions surrounding it**.

When you extend this taxonomy—commonly used in risk management, threat modeling, safety engineering, and legal or policy analysis—you capture the **mechanics, targets, conditions, and aftermath** of harm.

---

## 1\. The Extended Progression (From Source to Impact)

Here is what the complete sequence looks like when expanded logically from root cause to final consequence:

*   
* **A harmful intent / motive.** The underlying goal, ideology, negligence, or incentive driving the harm before any action occurs.  
* **A harmful actor.** *(Your starting point)* The person, group, organization, or autonomous system initiating or enabling the harm.  
* **A harmful capability / tool.** The weapon, software, resource, authority, or leverage the actor uses to carry out the harm.  
* **A harmful action.** *(Your starting point)* The specific behavior, choice, omission, or attack executed by the actor.  
* **A harmful vector / mechanism.** The pathway, channel, or chain of cause-and-effect through which the action travels to cause damage.  
* **A harmful event.** *(Your starting point)* The discrete incident, breach, accident, or crisis that occurs at a specific point in time.  
* **A harmful condition / vulnerability.** The pre-existing weakness, exposure, or lack of safeguards that allowed the event to succeed or escalate.  
* **A harmful situation.** *(Your starting point)* The ongoing state of danger, instability, or active crisis created by the event and conditions.  
* **A harmful target / victim.** The person, community, asset, or system experiencing the impact.  
* **A harmful outcome / consequence.** The measurable damage, injury, loss, or long-term systemic fallout resulting from the situation.

## 2\. How the Pattern Shifts as It Extends

Looking at the structure as a whole, extending the list reveals three distinct layers of analysis:

| Layer | Elements | Core Question Answered |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Agency & Execution** | Intent → **Actor** → Capability → **Action** | *Who is doing what, and with what means?* |
| **Occurrence & Context** | Vector → **Event** → Condition → **Situation** | *What is happening, how is it spreading, and what environment sustains it?* |
| **Impact & Aftermath** | Target → Outcome / Consequence | *Who or what is affected, and what is the resulting damage?* |

---

## 3\. Practical Example of the Extended Pattern

To see how this looks in practice, consider a **data breach**:

*   
* **Harmful intent:** Financial extortion.  
* **Harmful actor:** A ransomware group.  
* **Harmful capability:** Credential-harvesting malware.  
* **Harmful action:** Sending a spear-phishing email to an employee.  
* **Harmful vector:** Corporate email system and unpatched VPN.  
* **Harmful event:** Unauthorized access and encryption of company servers on Tuesday at 2:00 AM.  
* **Harmful condition:** Lack of multi-factor authentication (MFA) and outdated backups.  
* **Harmful situation:** Ongoing operational shutdown and active extortion negotiations.  
* **Harmful target:** The hospital network and its patients.  
* **Harmful outcome:** Delayed medical care, leaked patient records, and financial loss.
