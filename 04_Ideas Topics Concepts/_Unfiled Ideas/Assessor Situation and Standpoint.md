# Assessor Situation and Standpoint

## Why this is distinct
- Earlier cards say harm is assessed from a perspective, and the value card says perspectives must articulate values. This card adds the assessor's situation (material position, capacity, horizon) as a separate factor that shapes what looks like harm.

## Anticipated direction
- Record each assessor's situation alongside its values, not only its values.
- Test the same event from assessors in very different positions.

## Conceptual territory
- Extends [[Harm as Setback to What Is Valued - Perspective Value Articulation]] by adding standpoint as well as values.
- Relates to [[Diagnostic Model of Harm Assessment]], where the assessor is part of the diagnosis.

## Summary
- Harm looks very different to someone who earns a million dollars a year and is worth fifty billion, and to someone who is unemployed and eating scraps from a dumpster. Values alone do not explain this gap.
- Position also shapes capacity to see distant horizons. A person facing hunger may not be able to weigh cultural or long-range effects, and a wealthy assessor may not feel short-range ones.
- Standpoint should be recorded so that assessments can be compared fairly.

## Original wording
> "more than just what their values are what is their situation? Harm will look very different to somebody who earns a million dollars a year and is worth 50 billion then to somebody who's unemployed and eating scraps out of a dumpster. Also those two people have a very different capacity for looking at more distant horizons on the impact of particular cultural or beneficial effect"

## Real-world examples
- A rent increase felt as a crisis by a tenant and as a routine adjustment by a landlord with a portfolio.
- Climate risk assessed by a coastal farmer and by an insurer with diversified holdings.

## Why it matters to the book
- It explains why the book's characters see different harms in the same events. The standpoint is part of the story, not only its background.

## Open questions
- How is standpoint recorded: as a structured field, a narrative, or both?
- Does standpoint change which horizon an assessor is entitled to use?
- Can a well-placed assessor ever make a fair assessment of harm to someone poorly placed?
