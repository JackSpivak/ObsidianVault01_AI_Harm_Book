# HO-001 — Harm by Habituation and Captured Assent

Index: [[_Harm Ontology Bookmark Index]] · Top-level

## Why this is distinct
- Neither v1 nor the new layers name this harm: harm through **consensual, beneficial-seeming dependence**, where repeated assistance trains a person into deference and their judgment is exercised for them. It isn't coercion, deception or a bad choice. It's a bad *chooser* being formed.

## Anticipated direction
- A candidate new object of harm: **assent/judgment**, separate from cognition (open question in the redesign).
- A candidate mechanism: **habituation** (repetition forming disposition), alongside **manufactured desire** and **manufactured fear**.
- Possibly a central thesis for the book.

## Conceptual territory
- New in the harm model. It links to [[002 Scalability of Oversight and Control]] 002.II.a (rubber-stamping), which is the oversight-setting version. It draws on the first stress test ("agency hollowing") and the Classical Council (Aristotle on habituation, Marcus Aurelius on assent, Epicurus on empty desires).

## Summary
- AI's typical harm may not be an injury done to you but a self you are slowly trained into. Aristotle supplies the mechanism (habituation), the Stoics supply what gets damaged (assent), and Epicurus supplies the means (manufactured desire). A human who approves what they didn't understand has not been robbed. They have handed over the one thing that could not be taken from them.
