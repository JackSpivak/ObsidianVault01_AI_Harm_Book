Different disciplines frame this exact causal chain using distinct vocabularies that reflect what they care about most—whether that is **moral culpability**, **biological transmission**, **systemic failure**, or **dramatic conflict**.

Here are five distinct disciplinary framings, complete with their specific nouns and the narrative arc each stage tells.

---

1\. Public Health & Epidemiology (The Epidemiological Triad & Chain of Infection)

Public health strips away moral blame and frames harm as **contagion and susceptibility**. The narrative focuses on how an agent moves through an environment to compromise a host.

* **The Agent** *(Harmful Actor / Capability)* — *"There is a pathogen, toxin, or behavioral risk factor capable of causing disease."*  
* **The Reservoir** *(Harmful Condition)* — *"The agent lives and multiplies quietly in a source or environment."*  
* **The Portal of Exit & Mode of Transmission** *(Harmful Action / Vector)* — *"The agent leaves its source and travels through a specific pathway (airborne, contact, social network)."*  
* **The Portal of Entry** *(Harmful Event)* — *"The exact moment the agent breaches the body's or community's defenses."*  
* **The Susceptible Host** *(Harmful Target / Situation)* — *"An individual or population lacking immunity enters an active state of infection."*  
* **Morbidity / Mortality** *(Harmful Outcome)* — *"The resulting illness, disability, or loss of life."*

---

2\. Criminology & Routine Activity Theory (The Crime Triangle)

Developed by Lawrence Cohen and Marcus Felson, this framework argues that harm does not require a master plan—it happens when **three specific elements converge in time and space**.

* **The Motivated Offender** *(Harmful Intent \+ Actor)* — *"Someone with both the inclination and the ability to commit an offense."*  
* **The Absence of a Capable Guardian** *(Harmful Condition)* — *"The environment lacks surveillance, locks, witnesses, or oversight."*  
* **The Convergence** *(Harmful Event)* — *"The offender and target cross paths in an unguarded space at the exact same moment."*  
* **The Offense / Victimization** *(Harmful Action \+ Situation)* — *"The offender exploits the opportunity against the target."*  
* **The Suitable Target** *(Harmful Target)* — *"A person, object, or place that is visible, accessible, and valuable."*  
* **The Harm / Loss** *(Harmful Outcome)* — *"The resulting trauma, theft, or social disorder."*

---

3\. Systems Engineering & Resilience (Normal Accident Theory / Swiss Cheese Model)

Used in aviation, nuclear energy, and software reliability (pioneered by Charles Perrow and James Reason), this framing avoids blaming individuals and tells a story of **latent design flaws aligning into catastrophe**.

* **Latent Conditions / Pathogens** *(Harmful Condition)* — *"Hidden design flaws, poor maintenance, or bad incentives sit dormant inside the system."*  
* **Defensive Layers** *(Mitigations)* — *"Alarms, procedures, and barriers exist to stop errors, each with its own small 'holes' (weaknesses)."*  
* **Active Failure / Trigger** *(Harmful Action)* — *"An operator makes a slip, or a component fails under stress."*  
* **Trajectory of Opportunity** *(Harmful Vector \+ Event)* — *"The holes in every defensive layer momentarily line up, allowing the failure to pass straight through."*  
* **Systemic Excursion / Crisis State** *(Harmful Situation)* — *"The system loses stability and enters an uncontrolled state."*  
* **Catastrophic Failure / Loss** *(Harmful Outcome)* — *"Equipment destruction, environmental release, or loss of life."*

---

4\. Environmental Science & Disaster Risk Reduction (The Pressure and Release / PAR Model)

Used by the UN and disaster researchers, this framing explains how natural hazards turn into human disasters through **social and structural vulnerability**.

* **Root Causes** *(Harmful Intent / Systemic Condition)* — *"Distant economic, political, or historical factors distribute power and resources unevenly."*  
* **Dynamic Pressures** *(Harmful Vector)* — *"Rapid urbanization, deforestation, or lack of institutions channel those root causes into local risks."*  
* **Unsafe Conditions** *(Harmful Condition)* — *"People are forced to live in fragile housing, floodplains, or hazardous areas."*  
* **The Hazard** *(Harmful Actor / Trigger)* — *"A physical phenomenon—like an earthquake, storm, or drought—strikes."*  
* **The Disaster** *(Harmful Event \+ Situation)* — *"The hazard collides with unsafe conditions, overwhelming the community's ability to cope."*  
* **Devastation / Displacement** *(Harmful Target \+ Outcome)* — *"Loss of livelihoods, infrastructure collapse, and long-term recovery challenges."*

---

5\. Narrative Theory & Dramaturgy (Kenneth Burke’s Dramatistic Pentad)

In rhetoric, ethics, and investigative journalism, Kenneth Burke’s Pentad (later expanded to a Hexad) breaks any human conflict down into **a story of motive and accountability**.

* **The Agent** *(Harmful Actor)* — *"Who is the protagonist or perpetrator driving the story?"*  
* **The Purpose** *(Harmful Intent)* — *"Why are they doing it? What desire, fear, or ideology motivates them?"*  
* **The Agency** *(Harmful Capability / Vector)* — *"By what means, instruments, or methods do they carry it out?"*  
* **The Act** *(Harmful Action)* — *"What specific deed takes place?"*  
* **The Scene** *(Harmful Condition \+ Situation)* — *"When and where does it happen? What is the surrounding context or backdrop?"*  
* **The Patient / Counter-Agent** *(Harmful Target)* — *"Who endures the act or suffers its consequences?"*
