# Research, editing, and continuity rules

## Research
- Cite every factual claim: author/org, title, date, URL. Mark anything unverified as `(unverified)`.
- Never fabricate sources, quotes, figures, case details, or links. If unsure, say so.
- Distinguish: **fact** (sourced), **interpretation** (reasonable inference), **speculation** (for the book's purposes).
- Prefer primary sources (papers, incident reports, court records, official statements) over commentary.
- Save research as one note per source or topic in `02_Ideas Topics Concepts/Research/` using the Research template, and link it from the ideas/chapters it supports.

## Editing
- Levels: say which level you're working at — developmental (structure/argument), line (sentences/flow), or copy (grammar/consistency).
- Preserve the author's voice; follow `00_Claude_Admin/Style_Guide.md`.
- For manuscript edits, show a before/after or summary of changes unless asked to just apply them.
- Flag rather than fix anything that changes meaning, plot, or facts.

## Continuity checks
- Sources of truth, in order: `Canon_and_Continuity.md` → character/worldbuilding notes → outline → manuscript.
- Check: names/spellings, ages and dates, timeline order, who knows what when, technology rules, locations, character traits and voice.
- Report as a list: issue, where (wikilinks to both conflicting notes), suggested resolution. Don't change the manuscript without approval.

## Restructuring
- Propose the new structure first (outline or table of moves), wait for approval, then execute.
- After moving/renaming, check for and fix broken wikilinks.
