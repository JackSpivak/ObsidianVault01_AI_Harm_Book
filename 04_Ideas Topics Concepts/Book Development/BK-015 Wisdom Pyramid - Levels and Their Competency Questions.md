# Wisdom Pyramid - Levels and Their Competency Questions

## Why this is distinct
- The vault already has a short Wisdom Pyramid section in [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]]. That section names the tiers but not the questions each tier is competent to answer. This card adds the levels as a value-ordered pyramid and the questions each level can decide.

## Anticipated direction
- Fix the six levels and what each adds above the one below.
- Write, for each level, the questions it is competent to answer, and the questions it is not competent to answer (which pass up to a higher level).
- Test whether the questions form a competency test: a level should be able to decide its own questions and say when a question belongs above it.

## Conceptual territory
- Revisits the Wisdom Pyramid section in [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]], which lists only Data → Information → Knowledge → Wisdom.
- Related to [[BK-006 Communication Ladder - Elevator Pitch, Crowdfunding Pitch and Wisdom Pyramid]], which uses the pyramid for the book's messaging.
- Related to the competency questions in [[Harm Ontology v3 Test Summary]], which test whether the ontology answers its own questions.

## Summary
- The author prefers a pyramid of wisdom or enlightenment to a knowledge hierarchy, because knowledge sits near the bottom. Each level adds distinct value over the one below it.
- The levels, from the bottom up: data, information, knowledge, intelligence/understanding, wisdom, enlightenment.
- The questions are the competency test. A level is competent to decide the questions it can answer, and a question that needs a higher level has to pass up to it.

**Level questions (as given by the author)**
- **Knowledge:** "Could I do this?" or "How could I do this?"
- **Intelligence / understanding:** "Would I do this from a practical perspective?" and "Is it the right thing to satisfy goals and objectives of the requirement, situation, conditions and time?"
- **Wisdom:** "Should I do this?" and "Is it the right thing to do from the moral or ethical perspective?" Also: "Is the requirement itself morally proper, fit and just?" "Is the requirement well enough framed to even judge its potential outcomes from a moral or ethical perspective?" "Is it improperly situated within situations, conditions and time?"

**Levels without questions yet**
- **Data, information:** no questions have been given by the author. The questions "what is it?" and "who, when, where?" were drafted in [[BK-006 Communication Ladder - Elevator Pitch, Crowdfunding Pitch and Wisdom Pyramid]] as placeholders. They have not been confirmed and should not be treated as the author's.
- **Enlightenment:** no questions given. The vault has "wondering is the first step of enlightenment" in [[Principle - Ontology-Supported Inquiry of Care]]. Whether that is the enlightenment-level question is open.

## Connection to altitude representation
- **Likely relation:** the pyramid's higher levels ask from a wider frame, first practical goals, then moral and ethical framing. That resembles developmental altitude, which the Harm Ontology uses for how widely a value structure extends concern ([[Integral Altitude Scale]], [[Vocabulary Crosswalk - Concept Hubs and Discipline Schemes]]).
- **Not the same axis:** the pyramid orders what a level is competent to decide. Altitude orders how inclusive a value structure is. The ontology already keeps three scope axes apart: social size (`socialScope`), developmental altitude, and moral circle ([[Handoff - PerspectiveStructure 0.02]], [[PerspectiveStructure Proposal Draft 3.1]]). A pyramid level would be a fourth axis unless it can be shown to match one of these. This is open.
- **Whose ranking:** altitude scales encode their authors' views. The Kohlberg–Gilligan dispute and the Confucius and Laozi split show this. So any pyramid ranking needs the same `assessedBy` record that the Classical review proposed for altitude ([[HO-II.d.3 Hub and Spoke Pattern]]).
- **Status:** `developmentalAltitude` is still contested (S34 in [[Harm Ontology v3.1 — Feature Roadmap and Change Ledger]]). It is blocked until its citations are verified (V7). The pyramid should not depend on altitude until that is settled.
- **Where it could be represented:** if the pyramid becomes a vocabulary, it would be a concept scheme with each level as a hub, like the existing altitude hub ([[PerspectiveStructure 0.01]] for the perspective fields it would sit beside).

## Original wording
> "there is a notion in knowledge work that there is a hierarchy of knowledge. i prefer to think of it as a pyramid of wisdom or enlightenment because knowledge is somewhere near the bottom ... from bottom up: data, information, knowledge, intellegence/understanding, wisdom, enlightenment ... each hierarchical level represents a distinct value-add above the predecessor"
> "Knowledge Level might ask 'Could I do this? or How could i do this?' Intellegence/Understanding might ask 'Would I do this from a practical perspective? Is it the right thing to satisfy goals and objectives of the requirement, situation, conditions and time?' Wisdom might ask 'Should I do this? Is it the right thing to do from the moral or ethical perspective? Is the requirement itself morally proper, fit and just? Is the requirement well enough framed to even judge its potential outcomes from a moral or ethical perspective? Is it improperly situated within situations, conditions and time?'"
> "'The wood' (written as 'The wood should could and other comparable perspective questions') ... refers to a notion that distinguishes layers might be in the questions that could be answered from each levels perspectives."
- **Attribution:** the author
- **Source:** Obsidian Copilot chat, 2026-11-27 (the author's note on the wisdom pyramid, following the BK-010 review). The original transcription "The wood should could" is the author's; the subject is the pyramid, not a council.
- **External references:** the data-information-knowledge-wisdom (DIKW) hierarchy is standard in information science. The version with intelligence/understanding and enlightenment is the author's and has not been checked against a source.

## Real-world examples
- A clinician who knows how to run a test (knowledge), decides whether the test fits this patient's situation and time (understanding), and decides whether the treatment is right at all (wisdom).
- An engineer who knows how to build a bridge, decides whether it fits the budget and schedule, and decides whether it should be built where it is.

## Why it matters to the book
- It gives the book a way to say which kind of question each perspective (council) is competent to answer. A council of trade experts may answer "how", but the book needs a council that answers "should".
- It links to the book's claim that harm assessments come from perspectives: the question a perspective can answer depends on its level.

## Open questions
- Are the level questions a competency test, meaning a level must refuse what it cannot decide?
- Who asks the questions of data and information, and are they needed at all?
- Does enlightenment ask a different kind of question, or does it answer the questions below it?
- Does "is the requirement itself just?" belong to wisdom, or does it need its own level above?
