# HO-005 — Assessed Membership and Systematic Omission

Index: [[_Harm Ontology Bookmark Index]] · Top-level

## Why this is distinct
- It revises HO-003. The **events** of the past are settled, but **which of them count as members of a causal assembly is itself assessed.** Omitting causal factors through inadequate assessment is the **norm in reality, not an edge case**.

## Anticipated direction
- Test first, decide later. These proposals are **pending and deliberately not logged**:
  - split Assessment into Causal / Valence (/ Responsibility)
  - causal standards as a second family of standards (but-for, NESS, INUS, probability-raising, mechanistic, proximate; from memory, unverified)
  - reify `participatesIn` as a claim with a standard, confidence and assessor
- Find **re-attribution cases**: documented harms where successive investigations grew or changed the assembly over time. Model them as sequences of causal assessments. Candidates (from memory, unverified):
  - UK Post Office Horizon scandal (first choice)
  - Boeing 737 MAX
  - Challenger (normalisation of deviance)
  - peptic ulcers and *H. pylori*
  - COMPAS
- Work out an **anchor** against the regress: perspectives select and describe, but answer to evidence and traces.

## Conceptual territory
- New. NESS ("necessary element of a sufficient set") is almost literally the causal-assembly idea, which is a strong reuse candidate (unverified).

## Summary
- Membership is decided by causal standards, which disagree just as harm standards do. Since omission is normal, tests can't assume a complete "true" assembly. The model must treat "assessed assembly ⊂ unknown full assembly" as the default, and ask **which** factors get omitted and by whom, because omission is rarely random. New attack vectors:
  - **Engineered overdetermination:** redundant contributors, so that no one is a but-for cause.
  - **Causal-standard shopping:** including opacity as a shield against mechanistic causation.

## Caveats (Claude's own, after reflection)
- **Regress:** if membership is assessed, so is how factors are carved up. Without an evidence anchor the model slides into "everything is a view".
- The Causal/Valence split was proposed too fast; it needs testing first.
- Claims that the structure "blocks" traps are hypotheses until tested in [[HO-002 Ontology Stress-Test Suite]].

## Real-world examples
- Horizon: for years, subpostmasters were blamed while software faults and the Post Office's own conduct were omitted from the assembly (unverified; research first).

## Why it matters to the book
- Who gets left out of "the cause" is a site of power. The Horizon case is about a computer system that was kept out of the assembly.
