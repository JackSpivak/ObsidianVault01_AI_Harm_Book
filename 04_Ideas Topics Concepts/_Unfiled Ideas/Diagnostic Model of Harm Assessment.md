# Diagnostic Model of Harm Assessment

## Why this is distinct
- It asks how a harm is actually diagnosed, using medicine as the model, rather than only what harm is. No existing card treats assessment as a diagnostic process with criteria, differential diagnosis and sufficiency tests.

## Anticipated direction
- Define a harm diagnosis procedure: presenting case, signs and symptoms, differential (is this harm, mischief, or no harm?), tests, and a documented diagnosis with its confidence.
- Ask which characteristics are necessary and which are sufficient for each class of assessment, and expect the answer to vary by type.

## Conceptual territory
- Extends [[Harmfulness Is an Assessment - Storm, Gun and Reified Class]] by adding a procedure, not only a claim that harm is assessed.
- Overlaps with [[HO-II.a Assessment and Evaluative Standard]] on the standard; this card is about the procedure.

## Summary
- Harm assessment should follow a diagnostic process, as medicine does. The question is what characteristics are necessary and sufficient to conclude that harm is present, and this probably depends on the assessment type.
- The assessor's situation (wealth, position, horizon) is part of the diagnosis and must be recorded, not treated as neutral. See [[Assessor Situation and Standpoint]].
- Boundary cases such as dreams and mischief are the differential diagnoses that tell the procedure where its limits lie. See [[Dreams and Mischief - Boundary Tests of Harm]].

## Original wording
> "Is there a diagnostic process associated with an assessment when trying to determine if there is harm. Model for this would be medicine. How is harm assessed? What characteristics are necessary and or sufficient for an assessment. Probably varies by type of assessment"

## Real-world examples
- A clinician rules out a condition before diagnosing another. The harm equivalent is ruling out mischief, misfortune or ordinary friction before calling something harm.
- Medical screening (sensitivity and specificity) could show how often a harm test flags real harm and how often it flags false harm.

## Why it matters to the book
- It turns harm assessment into something teachable and checkable. Without a procedure, the book's central claim that harm is an assessment would stay abstract.

## Open questions
- Which characteristics are necessary and which sufficient for each assessment type (physical, economic, dignitary, ecological)?
- What is the equivalent of a diagnostic test for harm, and who may run it?
- What counts as a "false positive" for harm, and who pays for it?
