# Dreams and Mischief - Boundary Tests of Harm

## Why this is distinct
- These are boundary test cases, not harms in the usual sense. They test where the definition of harm stops: whether a dream can harm, and whether mischief is a small harm or only the attitude that makes harm possible.

## Anticipated direction
- Use dreams and mischief as edge cases in the stress-test suite, to see whether the ontology can say "no harm", "harm at a lower level" or "harm only in intention".
- Separate harm produced by an act from harm produced by an intention or disposition.

## Conceptual territory
- Feeds [[Diagnostic Model of Harm Assessment]] as differential-diagnosis cases.
- Relates to intention and attitude in [[HO-002 Ontology Stress-Test Suite]]. Territory is new as a test set.

## Summary
- Can dreams create harm? If a dream changes how someone acts or feels, the harm may be real but its carrier is internal. The question is whether the ontology has a place for harm that runs through experience alone.
- Is mischief lower-level harm, or is it an attitude that enables harm? Mischief may be a small setback to someone's valued state, or it may be the precursor without an effect yet. The ontology needs to represent both possibilities.

## Real-world examples
- Nightmares that cause measurable sleep loss and daytime distress (a harm with an internal carrier).
- Pranks that cause no lasting damage but humiliate a target (small harm or only mischief?).
- Mischief in the Trickster traditions, which often carries both harm and social correction.

## Why it matters to the book
- Boundary cases show whether the ontology is too narrow (it misses real harm) or too broad (it counts everything as harm). Both failures weaken the claim that harm is teachable.

## Open questions
- Does a harm need an external effect, or can an internal state alone count?
- Is intention a harm, a precursor, or a separate class (a "harm-enabling disposition")?
- At what point does mischief become harm, and is that a matter of degree or of kind?
