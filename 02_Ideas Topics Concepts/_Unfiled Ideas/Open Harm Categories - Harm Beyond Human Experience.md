# Open Harm Categories - Harm Beyond Human Experience

## Why this is distinct
- It's a claim about the limits of the harm ontology itself. It isn't about any one harm. Every category we have comes from human, physical-world experience, so the ontology needs deliberately empty slots for harms nobody can name yet.

## Anticipated direction
- Add placeholder ("TBD") categories to the harm ontology, either explicit or implied.
- Make this a stated thesis in the book: AI, along with digital and virtual realities, may change what harm *is*, not just add more of it.

## Conceptual territory
- New. It extends [[Interdisciplinary_Harm_Ontology_Compendium]] and [[003 Harm Ontology from Outlines]]. It also sets up [[The Self-Revising Harm Ontology]], where the AI fills in or rewrites the ontology itself.

## Summary
- The harm ontology should include empty, TBD categories, maybe shown openly and maybe left implicit, because AI development may produce kinds of harm that don't exist yet.
- Everything we know about harm comes from human and real-world experience. The book needs to stress this hard: AI and alternate digital and virtual realities may change the nature of harm in ways we've never anticipated.

## Original wording
> "First you need to have some harm categories that are simply empty sort of like TBD maybe explicit maybe not because with the development of AI unknown categories May emerge in the future. Everything we know about the nature of harm is based on human and real world experience. This is a critical point that needs to be emphasized in the book. AI and the existence of alternate digital and virtual realities make completely change the nature of harm and ways that we have never anticipated!!"
