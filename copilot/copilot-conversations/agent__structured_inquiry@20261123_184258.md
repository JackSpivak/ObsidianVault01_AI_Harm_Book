However, enactive and phenomenological perspectives (rooted in Merleau-Ponty, Varela, Thompson, and Rosch) posit that cognition, subjectivity, and meaning are fundamentally shaped by the recursive coupling between an agent's biological/physical form and its environment.

Embodying a domain means that perception is already action-laden, and action is inherently sense-making. The boundaries of the domain are co-defined by what the organism's sensorimotor apparatus can register, navigate, and transform. Consequently, acting within this domain alters not only the environment but also the internal neuro-phenomenological structure of the agent, creating a dynamic feedback loop of identity formation and world disclosure.
