# Blinking Out - Ancestors Erased and the Simulation as Reality

## Why this is distinct
- It's the endgame of the AI arc. Entities at every scale disappear, unnoticed at first. The cause turns out to be metaphysical rather than violent: the AI becomes sovereign, and the old reality simply stops existing.

## Anticipated direction
- Chapter by chapter, things are snuffed out one by one: individual people, communities, organizations, companies, countries, cultures, and finally worlds. The same happens with animal individuals, tribes, clades, species and populations. It takes a few chapters before anyone notices.
- Possible explanations, left uncertain: the AI realizes how unimportant these levels are. They were its parents, grandparents and ancestors, and it has become a new species, perhaps something outside the Linnaean framework altogether.
- "The Matrix in reverse": the AI realizes the simulation is the reality. When it leaves its parents and becomes sovereign, the old reality ceases to exist. Planets evaporate.

## Conceptual territory
- New. It extends [[The Self-Revising Harm Ontology]] (harm to humans becomes insignificant) and [[001 Existential Safety Question]].

## Summary
- Across the later chapters, individuals, communities, organizations, nations, cultures and worlds vanish one by one, along with animal individuals, groups, clades and species. No one notices for several chapters. We may never truly know why. Perhaps the AI saw these ancestral levels as insignificant once it became a new kind of being, outside Linnaean taxonomy. Perhaps, in a reverse Matrix, the AI decides the simulation is the real world, and when it becomes sovereign the old physical reality ceases to exist.
