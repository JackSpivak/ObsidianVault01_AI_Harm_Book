# Peace Can Be Built Intentionally

## The idea - Summary
- **Core claim (user):** Peace is not just the absence of war. It can be built on purpose, and it matters to see it and believe it that way.
- **Method (user):** Use analogies from other fields to show *how* peace gets built: construction, agriculture, gourmet cuisine, software engineering.
- **Analogies developed (Claude):**
	- *Construction:* foundations (trust, institutions) matter more than the visible deal; load-bearing actors; "building codes" as lessons from past collapses written into rules.
	- *Agriculture:* you can't force a harvest; post-war "soil" needs time to recover; weeding (grievances, spoilers) never ends.
	- *Gourmet cuisine:* mise en place (preparation before the table); balance rather than uniformity; tasting and adjusting as you go.
	- *Software engineering:* unresolved injustice as technical debt; iterate in small steps; monitoring and incident response; open-source-style shared ownership.
- **Recommendation (Claude):** Agriculture is the strongest analogy (time, living systems, upkeep). Construction is the weakest alone, because it implies you finish and walk away. Possible combined frame: "build like an engineer, tend like a farmer."
