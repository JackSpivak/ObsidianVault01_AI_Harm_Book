# Human-AI Complementary Strengths

## Why this is distinct
- It is a revisit of the "Human-AI Collaborative Councils" item already in [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]]. The new material is the question of how the two sets of strengths are brought into a productive relationship when goals, strategies and specifications are set.

## Anticipated direction
- List what AI offers (scale, pattern recognition, tireless iteration) and what humans offer (lived stakes, empathy, moral agency, embodied knowledge).
- Design a joint process where each side's strengths set the agenda for the other.

## Conceptual territory
- Revisits [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]]. It links to [[BK-011 Council Dialogue Mechanics - Question-Led Councils]] (how the councils work) and to [[BK-013 Cross-Model Questioning - AI Asks AI the Important Questions]].

## Summary
- Humans bring stakes, care, moral agency and lived experience. AI brings scale, breadth and tireless iteration.
- The open question is how to structure a council that includes both in a similar inquiry, and how to make the two sets of strengths work together.
- The output should be goals, strategies and specifications, not just opinions.

## Original wording
> "How to structure a council that involves humans in a similar inquiry.? What benefits or strengths can AI offer in this inquiry and what strengths to humans have to offer in this inquiry and how can lose to strengths be brought into productive relationship with each other in setting goals and strategies and specifications etc"

## Real-world examples
- Clinical teams where software proposes options and a clinician decides.
- Aviation crews that combine automated systems with human monitoring.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book is itself produced by this kind of pairing. Being explicit about the division of strengths makes the method visible.

## Open questions
- Which decisions must stay with humans, and why?
- How is an AI's contribution labelled so that it is not mistaken for a human's?
- Does the pairing change over the course of the book?
