# Communication Ladder - Elevator Pitch, Crowdfunding Pitch and Wisdom Pyramid

## Why this is distinct
- It sets out three ways of presenting the book at different lengths and for different audiences, and asks which question belongs at each level of the wisdom pyramid. No existing card covers the pitch or the pyramid.

## Anticipated direction
- Write an elevator pitch (under one minute), a crowdfunding pitch (the "kickstarter book", for supporters), and a synopsis.
- Map the pyramid levels (data, information, knowledge, wisdom) to the questions each level asks, and check which level the book reaches.

## Conceptual territory
- Relates to [[Questions Over Answers and the Book as Prompt]] (the synopsis as prompt) and to [[Stakes of the Inquiry]] (what the pitch must make clear).

## Summary
- Elevator pitch: one sentence on what the book asks and why it matters now.
- Kickstarter pitch: who the book is for, what supporters get, and why this inquiry needs them.
- Wisdom pyramid: at each level, the characteristic question. Data asks "what is it?", information asks "who, when, where?", knowledge asks "how?", and wisdom asks "should we, and what for?" (Standard form of the DIKW pyramid, to be checked.)
- The book should be able to say which level it works at.

## Real-world examples
- Crowdfunding campaigns that succeed by explaining the reader, not only the subject.
- Business plans that compress a book into a single problem statement.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book has to be sold, funded and understood at several lengths. A clear ladder keeps the framing consistent across them.

## Open questions
- Which one sentence says what the book is for?
- Does the kickstarter pitch need a different audience from the book itself?
- Which pyramid level is the book's intended destination?
