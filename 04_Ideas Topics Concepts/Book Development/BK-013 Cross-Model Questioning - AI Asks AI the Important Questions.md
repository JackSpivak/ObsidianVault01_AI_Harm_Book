# Cross-Model Questioning - AI Asks AI the Important Questions

## Why this is distinct
- It proposes that different AI models question each other's answers, and that a prototype conversation could test whether an AI system can help reason about stopping AI-caused harm. It also raises the paradox that the tool may not be able to reason.

## Anticipated direction
- Run a prototype conversation in which one model asks the important questions and another answers, with a human log.
- Compare what different models ask, and whether they ask questions the other would not.

## Conceptual territory
- Builds on the "AI-to-AI inquiry" and "paradox of reasoning tools" items in [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]] (the "Governance & Safety" section). Connects to [[BK-012 Questions Over Answers and the Book as Prompt]].

## Summary
- Can AI models ask one another the questions that matter, so that inquiry continues past one model's blind spots?
- The author asks whether it is possible to reason our way out of AI harm with a tool that cannot reason. The honest answer may be partial, and the test is to see where the reasoning breaks.
- A prototype conversation would be a concrete test and could become a chapter device.

## Original wording
> "Have various AI models ask the important questions from other AI models. Can we literally create a prototype AI conversation about how to stop AI harmed Is it possible to reason our way out of this with the tool that cannot reason"

## Real-world examples
- Red-teaming, where one model is used to probe another.
- Debate-style oversight proposals in AI safety, which depend on the verifier being able to judge the output.

## Why it matters to the book
- It makes the book's method part of its subject. If AI can ask useful questions of AI, the book can show it.

## Open questions
- Who chooses the models and is the choice itself a bias?
- How do we tell a useful question from a plausible-sounding one?
- What would count as the prototype having reasoned, rather than having produced text that reads like reasoning?
