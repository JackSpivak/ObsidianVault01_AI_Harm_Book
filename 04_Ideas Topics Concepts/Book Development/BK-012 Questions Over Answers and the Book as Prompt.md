# Questions Over Answers and the Book as Prompt

## Why this is distinct
- It makes questions the book's main product and tests the book by using its own synopsis as a prompt. It adds a final gap audit: what has not been covered, asked or elicited, and what the book fails to open.

## Anticipated direction
- Capture questions in the book's structure, not only in its back matter.
- Use the synopsis, or the whole book, as conversation history in a prompt, then ask what a smarter reader would ask next.
- Run a closing gap audit that lists omissions and missed openings.

## Conceptual territory
- Connects to [[BK-013 Cross-Model Questioning - AI Asks AI the Important Questions]] and to the meta-prompt idea in [[structured-inquiry-book-notes]].

## Summary
- Questions guide the direction of inquiry. Answers close it.
- The book should carry its questions in its structure, possibly as a question architecture in the book-creation platform.
- Two tests: "my favorite question: if someone smarter than I am would be asking what, what would they be asking?" and "what hasn't been covered, asked or elicited?"
- The gap audit also asks what shortcoming the book suffers from, and what opportunities it fails to open. "Flying under the radar" and "flying in the gaps" belong here: the book should look for what slips past attention.

## Real-world examples
- Good interviewers who prepare questions rather than conclusions.
- Scientific research programmes that record open questions as deliverables.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book is meant to open inquiry, not to close it. Testing it by its own synopsis is a direct check on that aim.

## Open questions
- How much of the synopsis should a prompt carry before it prejudges the answer?
- Who writes the gap audit, and is it separate from the book's authors?
- Where do the questions live in the final book: front matter, chapter ends, or a separate volume?
