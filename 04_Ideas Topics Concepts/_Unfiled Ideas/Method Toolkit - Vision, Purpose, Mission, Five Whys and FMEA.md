# Method Toolkit - Vision, Purpose, Mission, Five Whys and FMEA

## Why this is distinct
- It names the analytic tools the author wants the book and councils to use: a vision/purpose/mission framework, root-cause analysis with the five-question method, and failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA). Each is a method, not a topic.

## Anticipated direction
- Use vision, purpose and mission to state what the inquiry is for, and check each harm against them.
- Use five-whys root-cause analysis on each harm case to find the cause behind the cause.
- Use FMEA to list failure modes of the harm ontology and of the AI systems it describes, with severity, occurrence and detection scores.

## Conceptual territory
- FMEA is the basis of the "Council of FMEA failure mode and effect analysts" in [[BK-010 Council Roster Expansion]].
- Five-whys links to [[Causal Chains - Occurrents, Participants and Completeness Validation]].

## Summary
- Vision: the long-run state the inquiry hopes to bring about.
- Purpose: why the inquiry exists.
- Mission: what the inquiry does now, and for whom.
- Five whys: ask "why" five times to move from symptom to root cause.
- FMEA: enumerate failure modes, their effects, and their severity, occurrence and detectability.

## Real-world examples
- FMEA in aircraft design and in medical device approval.
- Five-whys in manufacturing defect investigations.

## Why it matters to the book
- The book makes claims about harm and failure. These tools give those claims a procedure, so they can be checked and reused.

## Open questions

- How are FMEA severity scores set for harms that the ontology cannot yet name (see [[Open Harm Categories - Harm Beyond Human Experience]])?
- Which of the three is the book's default?
